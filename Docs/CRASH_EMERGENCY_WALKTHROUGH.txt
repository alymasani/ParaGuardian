--------------------------------------------------------------------------------
A. WHAT CRASH DETECTION MEANS HERE
--------------------------------------------------------------------------------
The device measures:
  - forces on a box on a harness;
  - how that box rotates;
  - how high it is (barometer, LiDAR);
  - how fast it moves (GNSS).
It does NOT measure the pilot's body, consciousness or injuries. So the firmware can only say:

  "possible impact"   (ImpactCandidate)
       a sudden, corroborated force or rotation peak;
  "suspected crash"   (CrashSuspected)
       that peak, plus enough independent context (what happened before, what happened after),
       satisfying an explicit, documented rule.

A suspicion does not call anyone. It opens an INCIDENT whose first step is a COUNTDOWN that asks the
pilot "are you OK? cancel me". Only when nobody cancels is an SOS REQUEST created, and even then
whether help is reached depends on a phone and a network the firmware cannot see.

--------------------------------------------------------------------------------
B. WHY ACCELEROMETER MAGNITUDE IS NOT LINEAR ACCELERATION
--------------------------------------------------------------------------------
An accelerometer measures SPECIFIC FORCE f = a - g: everything except gravity's pull.
  - At rest, f points up with magnitude 1 g (9.81 m/s^2), even though nothing accelerates.
  - In free fall, f is about 0, even though the device accelerates at 1 g.
  - In a 60-degree banked turn, |f| is about 2 g.
So |f| is NOT "how hard the device is accelerating". The crash detector never subtracts gravity
from |f|; it uses |f| directly:
  - Impulse:   |f| peak far above its recent baseline (a sudden rise).
  - Quiet:     ||f| - g| small and rotation small (lying still). No attitude needed.
  - Free fall: |f| < 0.35 g for 150 ms (falling freely: a dropped device).
Gravity-compensated linear acceleration (Madgwick, Milestone 3) is used by the UKF, not here, so
gravity can never be removed twice.

--------------------------------------------------------------------------------
C. WHY IMPACT SPIKES ALONE ARE INSUFFICIENT
--------------------------------------------------------------------------------
Things that make a large |f| peak without a crash:
  - a hard but fine landing (3-6 g);
  - a spiral dive (3-4 g sustained);
  - a collapse and recovery (2-4 g);
  - turbulence (1.5-3 g);
  - dropping the device on a table (10+ g);
  - shaking it;
  - one IMU going faulty (any value).
Things that make a crash with a modest peak:
  - landing in a tree;
  - a slow slide down a slope;
  - an impact shorter than the 10 ms polling interval (missed or under-read).
A threshold alone would therefore both cry wolf and miss. The test crash_single_accelerometer_
threshold_never_suspects proves a 12 g spike on the ground never leads to a suspicion.

--------------------------------------------------------------------------------
D. MULTI-SENSOR EVIDENCE
--------------------------------------------------------------------------------
The detector counts NAMED, INDEPENDENT indicators (bits in crashev::*):

  impulse (IMU):  HighSpecificForce, SevereSpecificForce, HighAngularRate, SevereAngularRate,
                  SuddenForceChange, SuddenRateChange, ImuSaturation
  before impact:  AirborneAtImpact (flight phase), TakeoffAttempt, FastDescent (UKF sink rate),
                  GroundClosure (LiDAR close and closing), HighGroundSpeed (GNSS),
                  FreeFallBeforeImpact
  after impact:   Inactivity, AbnormalOrientation (attitude tilt), RepeatedImpacts,
                  PilotActivity (reported only), ResumedFlight (counter-evidence),
                  PostImpactDataMissing

Independence:
  - The impulse comes from the IMUs.
  - Context comes from the barometric UKF, LiDAR and GNSS.
  - Orientation comes from Madgwick.
  - Inactivity comes from the IMUs again, but in a different time window.
Missing sources are FLAGGED (crashflag::NavigationMissing, LidarMissing, GnssMissing,
AttitudeMissing). They are never assumed "OK" or "bad".

Corroboration across the three IMUs: the detector uses the SECOND-LARGEST per-IMU peak. With three
IMUs, one faulty IMU (spiking or dead) can neither create nor hide an impulse. With two IMUs it uses
the smaller. With one IMU the impulse can never be "strong".

--------------------------------------------------------------------------------
E. TEMPORAL WINDOWS
--------------------------------------------------------------------------------
All windows use real timestamps, compared as signed differences: int32(a - b), see
Processing/TimeBase.hpp. So they work across the uint32 millisecond wrap at 49.7 days. Frame counts
are never used: frames can be late, missing or repeated.

  baseline     EMA of |f| and |rate| with tau 0.5 s, from NON-impulse frames only
  candidate    500 ms after the first peak: collect the rest of the same pulse
  pre-window   3 s before the impact (from the history)
  recent       30 s: "was airborne recently" (landed then crashed still counts)
  observation  2 s minimum, 10 s maximum after the impact
  inactivity   4 s of quiet (short blips <= 200 ms tolerated)
  orientation  2 s of tilt >= 60 degrees while not rotating fast
  resumed      3 s of GNSS >= 6 m/s while airborne and clear of the ground

The HISTORY is 32 slots x 250 ms = 8 s. Each slot keeps:
  - min vz, min clearance, max closure, max GNSS speed;
  - "airborne" and "takeoff candidate" flags.
It is fixed memory, and the scan is bounded (32 slots, only when an impulse starts).

--------------------------------------------------------------------------------
F. STATE MACHINES
--------------------------------------------------------------------------------
Crash detector:  Monitoring -> ImpactCandidate -> PostImpactObservation -> CrashSuspected
                 (or Rejected) -> Cooldown -> Monitoring;  Unavailable when no IMU data.
Emergency:       Idle -> Countdown -> SosPending <-> AwaitingPhoneAck -> AwaitingDelivery ->
                 Delivered | DeliveryUnknown | DeliveryFailed;  Cancelled;  Unavailable.

Why state machines? Each state says exactly which inputs matter and which transitions are allowed.
A test can drive any path, and the state goes into g_systemHealth so a debugger shows it. Every
transition is time-driven or input-driven, never hidden in a counter.

--------------------------------------------------------------------------------
G. HYSTERESIS AND COOLDOWNS
--------------------------------------------------------------------------------
  - Impulse baseline: a peak must exceed its own recent average by 2 g (force) or 250 dps
    (rotation). Slow load changes never trigger.
  - Quiet tolerance: a 200 ms blip does not reset the inactivity timer. A longer movement does.
  - Rejected is displayed 1 s.
  - After a suspicion the detector holds CrashSuspected 60 s, then spends 30 s in Cooldown. Further
    impacts are counted but can't open a second suspicion. Then it re-arms.
  - The emergency manager re-arms after a cancellation (10 s), or after an explicit
    ResetIncident once an SOS outcome is known.
  - Landing warnings keep their own hysteresis and cooldown from Milestone 5.

--------------------------------------------------------------------------------
H. CRASH SUSPICION VERSUS EMERGENCY ESCALATION
--------------------------------------------------------------------------------
Two classes, two tasks, one direction of data:
  - CrashDetector (SensorProcessingTask) decides SUSPICION. It knows nothing about SOS.
  - EmergencyManager (EmergencyAlertTask) decides ESCALATION. It latches a suspicion by the
    detector's suspicionSerial counter (a counter, not a level, so a short-lived state can't be
    missed), opens an incident and starts the countdown.
A suspicion during an open incident only increments additionalImpacts, so there is never a second
incident. If the detector withdraws its suspicion (flight resumed) during an AUTOMATIC countdown,
the manager cancels it, unless the pilot already confirmed it by pressing SOS.

--------------------------------------------------------------------------------
I. COUNTDOWN AND CANCELLATION
--------------------------------------------------------------------------------
  - The countdown is a DEADLINE (now + 60 s automatic, + 5 s manual; placeholders), checked by
    tick(now). There is no delay, sleep or loop.
  - Boundary: a cancel ISSUED at or before the deadline wins. Expiry happens strictly after the
    deadline (tick: reached(now, deadline + 1)). The task handles commands BEFORE ticking.
  - Late processing: a cancel issued at 30 s but processed after a 75 s scheduler gap is still
    honoured, because the command carries its issue time (test em_43_44).
  - Late expiry: if the task was delayed, the expiry happens at the next tick. The lateness is
    recorded (countdownOverrunMs, CountdownOverrun flag) and never hidden.
  - Cancelling needs the incident id: you can't cancel "whatever is running".
  - After the request has LEFT the device, a cancel cannot recall it. The manager sends a
    cancellation NOTICE (same incident, next revision, kind Cancel) and reports CancelNotice
    Pending, then Sent, then Accepted or Failed. "Accepted" means only that the phone accepted the
    notice. Contacts may already have been alerted.

--------------------------------------------------------------------------------
J. MANUAL SOS
--------------------------------------------------------------------------------
A ManualSos command needs no crash evidence:
  - in Idle it opens an incident with a 5 s guard countdown (an accidental press can still be
    cancelled);
  - during an automatic countdown it confirms immediately;
  - while sending it has no effect (idempotent);
  - after a final outcome it re-sends the same incident with a fresh position.
Commands come from abstract sources (HardwareInput, PhoneApp, Diagnostic, Test) through
EmergencyAlertTask::submitCommand(). No button is assumed to exist.

--------------------------------------------------------------------------------
K. INCIDENT IDs AND IDEMPOTENCY
--------------------------------------------------------------------------------
"Idempotent" means doing the same thing twice has the same effect as doing it once.
  - Every incident has an id (1, 2, ...; never 0). Every request revision has a number (1, 2, ...).
  - The phone deduplicates by (device, incidentId), so re-sending after a disconnect is safe.
  - Commands carry (source, sequence): the same pair twice is a duplicate.
  - Reports carry (incidentId, revision):
      wrong incident       -> ignored;
      revision never sent  -> ignored;
      same stage again     -> duplicate, ignored.
    So a duplicate acknowledgment can never create a new incident.
  - Without persistence (firmware today) ids restart after a reset. The request says so
    (IncidentIdVolatile).

--------------------------------------------------------------------------------
L. BLE VERSUS PHONE VERSUS CONTACT ACKNOWLEDGMENT
--------------------------------------------------------------------------------
  Created             the firmware built the request          (nothing left the device)
  QueuedForTransport  submit() accepted it into a buffer       (not sent)
  TransferredToPhone  the BLE write completed                  (the radio delivered bytes)
  AcceptedByPhone     the phone APP said "parsed and accepted" (the app has it)
  PhoneSendAttempted  the app started an SMS / call / upload   (an attempt)
  DeliveryConfirmed   the app REPORTED a delivery confirmation (an SMS delivery report is not a
                      human reading it, and the firmware cannot verify the report)
Each stage is a different fact from a different party. The manager:
  - keeps reportedStages (which stages were actually reported);
  - flags ProvenanceIncomplete when a later stage arrives without an earlier one;
  - uses DeliveryUnknown when the phone accepted but never reported an outcome.
None of these is ever turned into success.

--------------------------------------------------------------------------------
M. RETRY / BACKOFF
--------------------------------------------------------------------------------
  - An ATTEMPT is a submit() the transport accepted.
  - If the phone doesn't accept within 10 s, the attempt failed.
  - The next attempt waits 2 s, 4 s, 8 s, 16 s, 32 s, then 60 s (exponential backoff), so a
    struggling link is not flooded.
  - 8 attempts make one cycle. Then DeliveryFailed (RetriesExhausted), with an alert to the pilot.
  - A reconnect starts at most 2 more cycles; a manual SOS always re-sends.
  - Waiting for a link is NOT an attempt: the request stays pending forever, with
    TransportUnavailable raised. A request is never silently dropped.
Measured in the host test: 12.0 s and 14.0 s between the first attempts; exhaustion after 262 s.

--------------------------------------------------------------------------------
N. EVENT QUEUES
--------------------------------------------------------------------------------
SafetyEvent (40 B) is a TRANSITION, not a sample: Raised, Escalated, Deescalated, Cleared, Notice.
The processing task's SafetyEventQueue<16> is bounded and priority-aware. When full, it drops the
oldest lowest-severity event, and never a critical one for a lower one. Milestone 6 adds:
  - its first CONSUMER: the dispatcher in EmergencyAlertTask;
  - crash events in the same queue (same emitter, one id sequence);
  - the manager's events in a task-local queue (its own emitter, its own id sequence).
The SOS state is NOT carried by events. The dispatcher reads EmergencyStatus directly, and the
pending request lives in PendingSosRecord, so a full advisory queue can't lose an SOS.

--------------------------------------------------------------------------------
O. ALERT PRIORITIES
--------------------------------------------------------------------------------
One table, AlertPriorityPolicy.hpp:
  SOS countdown 100 > crash alarm 95 > communication failure 92 > SOS active 90
  > landing Critical 80-84 > Warning 60-64 > Caution 40-44 > Advisory 20-24.
The highest priority drives the audio, and separately the LED; ties go to the most recent alert.
  - Acknowledgment silences the AUDIO of an acknowledgeable alert episode. The LED keeps showing
    it, and escalation sounds again.
  - The SOS countdown and the crash alarm cannot be silenced: you cancel them instead.

--------------------------------------------------------------------------------
P. AUDIO / LED SCHEDULING
--------------------------------------------------------------------------------
AudioAlertScheduler turns "play pattern X" into IAudioAlert::request() calls, one per beep, each
with its own duration, at the right times. update(now) runs every 20 ms and never waits.
  - Preemption cancels the current tone and starts the new pattern from its first beep.
  - A late update plays the next due beep: no burst of missed beeps.
  - A backend that refuses means state Unavailable, retried once per second.
LedPatternScheduler forwards only pattern CHANGES and computes the reference on/off level
levelAt(pattern, phaseMs). That function is the timing spec for a future GPIO driver.
The firmware backends are Unconfigured (no pins known): they report Unavailable honestly.

--------------------------------------------------------------------------------
Q. FREERTOS TASK DESIGN
--------------------------------------------------------------------------------
  SensorAcq      40  10 ms   reads sensors (unchanged)
  EmergencyAlert 36  20 ms or on notification   NEW
  SensorProc     32  per frame   + CrashDetector after SafetyMonitor
  Health         16  1 s     + 45 new health fields

Why 36? Above processing, so if processing overruns, the countdown, a cancel or a retry still
runs on time. Below acquisition, so sampling is never delayed. Its work is tiny and bounded
(estimated < 0.1 ms per wake). The static_asserts in TaskConfig.hpp lock this ordering in.

Why not inside SensorProc? A countdown inside processing stops when frames stop, and a crash may
break a sensor or stall processing.

Synchronisation, everything non-blocking:
  - latest-value mailboxes for every struct crossing tasks;
  - one producer and one consumer per queue;
  - locked bounded inboxes for commands and reports;
  - critical sections only around copies of <= 188 bytes.
ulTaskNotifyTake() with a 20 ms timeout is the only blocking call, and it is the task's idle wait
(no busy loop).

--------------------------------------------------------------------------------
R. SIMULATION AND FAULT INJECTION
--------------------------------------------------------------------------------
Tests/sim/CrashSimulator.hpp layers on the Milestone 5 simulator (true kinematics, terrain, LiDAR
ray-tracing, the REAL SafetyMonitor). Ground contact is DETECTED from the truth, and impacts are
attached to it. Each IMU is sampled at its own instant within the 10 ms cycle (short pulses can be
missed). Values clip at 16 g with a saturation flag.

Faults injected:
  - GNSS, LiDAR or barometer loss;
  - one IMU absent, offset or spiking;
  - total IMU dropout;
  - processing silence;
  - clock wrap;
  - 75 s scheduler gaps;
  - queue overflow;
  - transport down, phone disconnect, ack loss, wrong incident, delivery failure;
  - resets with mock persistence;
  - audio or LED absent.
Parameters were swept on a TUNING set, then FROZEN, then run on a separate HELD-OUT set. Two
honest ambiguity tests show cases the algorithm can't separate (hard landing vs crash).

--------------------------------------------------------------------------------
S. HOW TO DEFEND THE IMPLEMENTATION IN A CAPSTONE REVIEW
--------------------------------------------------------------------------------
Likely questions and short answers:

Q: "Why not just a 4 g threshold?"
A: Section C. The test crash_single_accelerometer_threshold_never_suspects, and the spiral, drop,
   shake and hard-landing scenarios.

Q: "Why not run it on the UKF output?"
A: The UKF smooths and gates; it would remove the impulse. We use per-IMU peaks before smoothing.
   The test real_pipeline_carries_impact_peaks_and_saturation_before_any_smoothing shows a batch
   with the impact in the middle sample is kept, and a saturated sample is flagged even though the
   voter drops it.

Q: "What if an IMU fails during the crash?"
A: Second-largest corroboration. Scenarios 20, 21 and 21b.

Q: "What if all sensors die after impact?"
A: Explicit policy: StrongImpactDataLoss (scenario 22). If the processing task itself dies, the
   manager's DetectorSilence rule applies.

Q: "Is a suspicion a crash?"
A: No. It is a reason to ask the pilot (countdown). Section 4.3 of the validation report shows two
   cases we deliberately cannot separate.

Q: "How do you know the SOS was delivered?"
A: We don't. We know what the phone app REPORTED, stage by stage, with provenance. DeliveryUnknown
   exists for exactly this.

Q: "What if the queue overflows?"
A: Critical events are kept, losses are counted as id gaps, and the SOS state never travels
   through the queue.

Q: "Does it work on the board?"
A: Builds: yes, 0 errors and 0 warnings. Hardware: not tested. Transport, audio, LED and
   persistence are unconfigured on purpose, until the schematic and protocols are verified.

Q: "Where do the numbers come from?"
A: Physically motivated placeholders (design section 5), swept in simulation (report 4.1). Real
   recordings are the next step (HARDWARE_INTEGRATION_TODO.md section 16).

Weak points to admit up front:
  - synthetic data only;
  - 100 Hz polling;
  - 16 g saturation;
  - hard landing vs crash ambiguity;
  - dragging could look like resumed flight;
  - no persistence on target;
  - countdown durations not human-factors validated.

--------------------------------------------------------------------------------
U. WORKED SCENARIO 1: normal flight -> impact -> suspicion -> countdown -> SOS -> phone -> outcome
--------------------------------------------------------------------------------
(Numbers from test e2e_64_suspected_crash_countdown_sos_phone_ack_delivery; times in simulation
seconds.)

 0.0 s   On launch, still. Phase Unknown -> Ground. CrashDetector Monitoring. Manager Idle. LED slow
         green (normal).
10-13 s  Take-off run; the climb starts. The phase becomes TakeoffCandidate, then Flying (Milestone 5).
         The history slots start recording "airborne", vz, clearance and GNSS speed.
33-40 s  Glide at -1.2 m/s, 10 m/s. No impulse: the IMU peaks stay near 1 g, and the EMA baseline
         follows them.
40.0 s   Collapse: the sink becomes -9 m/s. The UKF vz drops; the LiDAR clearance falls and the
