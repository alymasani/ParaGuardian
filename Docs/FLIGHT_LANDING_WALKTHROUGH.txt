A. WHAT A FLIGHT-STATE MACHINE IS
----------------------------------
A state machine is a variable that can take a few named values (states),
plus rules saying when it may move from one to another (transitions).
Ours is pg::FlightPhase (Flight/FlightStateTypes.hpp):

  Unknown, Ground, TakeoffCandidate, Flying, Approach, LandingCandidate, Landed

Why bother? Later code asks "is the pilot flying?" constantly:
  - a descent warning makes no sense while the device sits in a car;
  - a crash detector must know whether a big jolt happened in flight or
    on a table.
The state machine answers once, carefully, so that every consumer does not
invent its own answer.

The two "Candidate" states are deliberate. They mean "something suggests a
change, I am checking". A phase only becomes Flying or Landed after the
evidence has held for a while.


B. WHY ONE THRESHOLD IS INSUFFICIENT
--------------------------------------
Tempting: "if vertical speed < -0.5 m/s, we are flying". Every simple rule
fails on a normal day:
  - a paraglider in lift flies LEVEL or climbs (vertical speed 0 or positive);
  - in a strong headwind it can fly at almost 0 km/h ground speed;
  - a pilot walking up a launch hill gains 100 m of altitude;
  - a car ride is fast;
  - a device dropped on the floor falls at 4 m/s for a moment.
Each sensor is fooled by something. So the detector collects several
independent pieces of EVIDENCE (section E) and needs more than one, or one
strong one, held for long enough.


C. HYSTERESIS
--------------
Hysteresis means different thresholds for switching ON and switching OFF.
Example from FlightStateConfig:

  approachClearance_m     = 30   Flying   -> Approach when clearance < 30 m
  approachExitClearance_m = 45   Approach -> Flying   when clearance > 45 m

Without the 15 m gap, a pilot soaring at 30 m would flip between Flying and
Approach every time the LiDAR noise (about 30 cm at that range) crosses 30 m.
The warnings use the same idea. ExcessiveDescent enters Caution at 3.5 m/s
of sink and stays until the sink is below 3.0 m/s (descentHysteresis_mps =
0.5). The helper is graded() in LandingSafetyAnalyzer.cpp:

    const float t = (current >= levels[i]) ? thresholds[i] - hyst : thresholds[i];
    if (x > t) out = levels[i];

"If I am already at this level, the bar to stay is lower."


D. DWELL TIMERS
----------------
A dwell (or persistence) timer requires a condition to be true
CONTINUOUSLY for some time before acting. The detector uses a tiny helper:

    static uint32_t persist(bool cond, bool& active, uint32_t& since, uint32_t nowMs)
    // returns how long `cond` has been continuously true (0 = false now)

Examples:
  - takeoffConfirmMs  = 3000: two airborne sources for 3 s -> Flying;
  - landingConfirmMs  = 5000: LandingCandidate held for 5 s -> Landed;
  - descentPolicy.raiseMs = 1000: sink above the threshold for 1 s before
    a warning is raised.

Dwell timers trade SPEED for ROBUSTNESS. The sweep (validation report
section 9) shows it directly: takeoffConfirmMs of 1 / 3 / 6 s gives takeoff
detection 2.3 / 4.3 / 7.3 s after lift-off.

A lesson from testing: a dwell timer is only as good as its input. The first
"altitude steady" test used ONE noisy vertical-speed sample per frame. With
0.12 m/s noise it failed about 1 % of the time, and each failure RESTARTED the
2 s landing timer. Landing detection took 31 s. Judging steadiness over the
whole 8 s window fixed it (15 s).


E. EVIDENCE-BASED TRANSITIONS
------------------------------
Evidence is a set of bits computed every update (namespace flightev).

  AIRBORNE                                 GROUND
  LidarHigh        clearance > 2.5 m       LidarNearGround  clearance <= 2.5 m
                   (the strong one)
  GnssFast         ground speed > 6 m/s    GnssSlow         ground speed < 1 m/s
  VerticalMotion   |vz| > 0.8 m/s for 3 s  AltitudeSteady   8 s window: spread <= 2 m,
                                                            net rate < 0.3 m/s
  AltitudeDeparture |h - h_ground| > 15 m  Still            IMU quiet (RMS accel < 0.4,
                                                            rate < 0.05 rad/s)

Key rules (FlightStateDetector::update):
  - Ground -> TakeoffCandidate: any airborne evidence.
  - TakeoffCandidate -> Flying:
      * 2 sources or LidarHigh for 3 s, OR
      * 1 source for 15 s, but never AltitudeDeparture alone (walking
        uphill).
  - A valid LiDAR that sees the ground (LidarNearGround) VETOES takeoff
    confirmation: running fast with the wing up is not flying.
  - No airborne evidence for 2 s -> back to Ground ("aborted takeoff").
  - Flying -> LandingCandidate: 2 ground sources and NO airborne evidence for
    2 s; then 5 s more -> Landed.
  - Nothing usable at all -> the phase is HELD and the timers are frozen.

The "support" field (None / Weak / Moderate / Strong) counts the agreeing
sources. It is NOT a probability: nobody has measured how often each
combination is right on real flights. Calling it "87 % sure" would invent
evidence that does not exist.


F. ALTITUDE IS NOT CLEARANCE
-----------------------------
Three different "heights" exist on the device:
  UKF altitude_m   relative to where the barometers were calibrated (the launch)
  GNSS height      relative to sea level
  LiDAR clearance  distance down to the terrain under the beam

After launching from a 1 000 m mountain and gliding over a valley, the UKF
can say "-300 m" while the valley floor is 700 m below. The code never uses
altitude as clearance. Altitude only feeds the evidence "the altitude moved
a lot since takeoff" (AltitudeDeparture) and "the altitude stays put"
(AltitudeSteady).


G. LIDAR GEOMETRY
------------------
The TF02-Pro shoots one beam and reports the distance r to whatever it hits,
measured ALONG THE BEAM. To know how far below we are, we need the beam's
direction in the world:

  1. The beam direction in body axes is configuration:
       GroundClearanceConfig::beamBody = (0, 0, 1)   // body FRD z = down when level
     This is a PLACEHOLDER (no hardware). mountingVerified = false, and every
     output is flagged MountingUnverified.
  2. The attitude quaternion q = q_ENU_from_body rotates it into the world:
       d_ENU = q (x) b (x) q*        (quat::rotate in GroundClearanceEstimator::beamEnu)
  3. ENU z points UP. A downward beam has d_ENU.z < 0. The "projection
     factor" is
       p = -d_ENU.z = cos(angle between the beam and straight down)
  4. The vertical distance to the point the beam hit is
       clearance = r * p

Why the minus sign: ENU's z is up, the beam goes down, so d_ENU.z is
negative, and the clearance must be positive.


H. SLANT RANGE AND TILTED SENSORS
----------------------------------
In a 30-degree bank over flat ground at 10 m, the beam is tilted 30 degrees
from vertical. It hits the ground at slant range r = 10 / cos 30 = 11.55 m.
Reporting 11.55 m as "clearance" would be 15 % wrong. The projection gives
11.55 * 0.866 = 10.00 m (test gc_17_18_19: exactly 10.000).

Limits:
  - Tilt > 40 degrees (maxBeamTiltDeg): REFUSED (status BeamGeometry). The
    point hit is then more than 0.84 r away sideways, and small attitude
    errors dominate.
  - Beam horizontal or pointing up: refused. A clearance is never invented.
  - Tilt > 20 degrees: still valid but flagged HighTilt.

Uncertainty (clearanceSigma_m) adds three effects:
  range noise times p, (r sin(tilt) times the attitude error), and a
  second-order term:
      sigma_c^2 = (sigma_r p)^2 + (r sin(theta) sigma_theta)^2 + (0.5 r p sigma_theta^2)^2
  With the mounting unverified, 5 degrees of mounting uncertainty is added to
  sigma_theta. That is why the worked example in Q reports 0.54 m: mostly
  "we don't know exactly where the LiDAR points".


I. TERRAIN EFFECTS
-------------------
r * p is the height above the ILLUMINATED POINT. It equals the height above
the ground directly beneath you only if the ground is flat. On a slope the
beam lands uphill or downhill of you.

From the test, 15 m above the slope, 20-degree bank:
    slope 10 deg: +1.03 m or -0.90 m;  20 deg: +2.29 / -1.75 m;  30 deg: +3.99 / -2.60 m
The estimate equals the illuminated-point truth to < 1 mm. The error is the
ASSUMPTION, not the arithmetic, so it is flagged (FlatTerrainAssumed) rather
than "corrected" with a slope a single beam cannot see.

Sudden changes (cliffs, bushes, buildings, water) are REAL more often than
not. The estimator therefore does NOT smooth the clearance. A 12 m cliff
drop shows up in the next sample (32.2 m vs 32 m truth). A jump larger than
max(1.5 m, 10 %) only:
  - sets StepChange;
  - restarts the closure-rate history, so a slope fitted across the step
    cannot invent a fake closure rate.


J. DESCENT-RATE DETECTION
--------------------------
Two different "rates" exist:
  - UKF vertical speed vz: movement relative to the AIR / barometric
    reference, positive up.
  - LiDAR closure rate: how fast the distance to the TERRAIN shrinks,
    positive = approaching.
Flying level (vz = 0) towards a hill gives closure > 0. Descending over a
valley that falls away faster gives closure < 0. Test gc_23: level flight at
9 m/s over terrain rising 1:5 -> UKF vz 0, LiDAR closure 1.99 m/s (truth 1.8).

The closure rate is the least-squares SLOPE of the clearance over the last
400 ms (GroundClearanceEstimator::fitRate):
  - at least 6 samples spanning 200 ms, timestamps used (variable dt);
  - a residual RMS > 0.5 m (spiky data) -> no rate;
  - latency ~ half the window (200 ms);
  - noise: at 30 m the range noise is ~0.3 m, so the rate noise is ~0.3-0.4
    m/s. The reported sigma (0.33 m/s mean) says so.
Why not differentiate two samples? (30.3 - 30.0) / 0.01 s = 30 m/s of pure
noise. One sample cannot give a rate at all.

ExcessiveDescent (navigation) warns at 3.5 / 6 / 10 m/s of sink (Caution /
Warning / Critical). Only if the UKF velocity is fresh and its sigma <= 1 m/s.
If it is not, NavigationDegraded says why (test C41).


K. TIME TO CONTACT
-------------------
  TTC = clearance / closure_rate        (seconds; constant closure assumed)

Computed only when:
  - clearance and rate are valid;
  - closure > 0.3 m/s;
  - closure - 2 sigma > 0 (really approaching, not noise);
  - TTC <= 60 s.
Otherwise ttcValid = false and timeToContact_s = NaN: "no contact predicted".
It is never 0 (that would mean "impact now") and never infinity.

It is an APPROXIMATION. A flare or a turn changes the closure, and so does
terrain shape. Adding the UKF vertical acceleration was considered and
rejected: it is acceleration relative to the air, not to uneven terrain.


L. WARNING SEVERITY
--------------------
  Info < Advisory < Caution < Warning < Critical   (SafetyEvent.hpp)

  Advisory  awareness only: LowGroundClearance on every final approach;
            TerrainClearanceUnavailable; NavigationDegraded
  Caution   something is unusual: sink > 3.5 m/s; closure > 4 m/s;
            fast descent with no LiDAR
  Warning   act: sink > 6 m/s; TTC < 4 s; low and descending fast
  Critical  urgent: sink > 10 m/s; TTC < 2 s; < 6 m and > 3.5 m/s down

These numbers are PROVISIONAL. There is no universally safe descent rate.
A big-ears descent is ~3-4 m/s on purpose; a spiral dive can exceed 15 m/s.


M. WARNING PRIORITIES AND EVENTS
---------------------------------
Each warning has a tracker (LandingSafetyAnalyzer::step). The desired level
is computed every frame, but EVENTS are published only on changes:

  Raised -> (Escalated / Deescalated)* -> Cleared     one episodeId throughout

  - raise needs persistence (e.g. 1 s), escalation less (0.5 s), Critical
    least (half / none);
  - relaxing (de-escalate or clear) needs persistence too (2 s), so warnings
    do not flicker;
  - cooldown (e.g. 20 s): if the same warning comes back at the same or a
    lower severity soon after clearing, it is tracked (visible in the
    snapshot) but NOT announced again. Higher severity or Critical is always
    announced (test C46).

A held warning produces ONE event, not one every 10 ms (test
ls_events_only_on_transitions). Consumers that need "still active" read the
SafetySnapshot.

Queue priority (SafetyEventQueue): when full, the oldest LOWEST-severity
event is dropped to make room for a more severe one. A Critical is never
lost to lower-priority traffic (2 000 events, 10x slow consumer: 0 critical
dropped).


N. FAULT HANDLING
------------------
Principles:
  1. Missing is never zero. Invalid numbers are NaN with a status
     (ClearanceStatus, *Valid flags).
  2. Missing is never "safe". A warning whose input disappears is HELD for
     3 s (evidenceHoldMs), flagged degraded, then released.
  3. A missing sensor is reported once (TerrainClearanceUnavailable,
     NavigationDegraded), not repeated.
  4. Outages never cause Landed: with no input at all the phase is held.
  5. Contradictions are resolved by the sensor that measures the right thing.
     Terrain hazards use the LiDAR closure rate; only when it is missing is
     navigation -vz used, flagged DegradedEvidence. A navigation-only descent
     of -6 m/s with a steady LiDAR raises ExcessiveDescent but no terrain
     hazard.
  6. Unverified hardware is visible everywhere: LidarMountingUnverified,
     ImuMountingUnverified, ModelPlaceholder (from the UKF), Unvalidated (all
     thresholds).

Tested faults:
  - all IMUs (attitude invalid);
  - both barometers (UKF unavailable);
  - UKF degraded;
  - GNSS lost;
  - LiDAR lost;
  - +30 m altitude jump;
  - stale timestamps;
  - wrong mounting;
  - queue overflow.
In every case: no false landing and no Warning-or-higher.


O. FREERTOS INTEGRATION
------------------------
SensorProcessingTask::Run() gained one line after the navigation update:

    safetyOut_.publish(safety_.update(pg::safetyInputFrom(work_, attitude_.latest(), navigation_.latest())));

  - No new task. The safety update costs ~126 ns on a laptop, estimated
    40-130 us on the STM32. A separate task would cost a stack (>= 1 KB), a
    queue and copies, and would see data later.
  - Order inside SafetyMonitor::update(): clearance -> flight phase ->
    phase notice -> warnings. Each uses THIS frame's attitude and navigation,
    so nothing is stale or circular.
  - Outputs:
      * safety()       SafetySnapshotMailbox (copy under a critical section);
      * safetyEvents() SafetyEventQueue<16> (one consumer task pops).
  - Health: g_systemHealth gained 24 fields (phase, clearance, warnings,
    counters, queue statistics). Watch them in STM32CubeIDE Live
    Expressions.
  - Memory: +3 088 B static RAM, +11 584 B flash (Release), no heap. The
    deepest stack chain (navigation) is ~0.97 KB of the 4 KiB processing
    stack.


P. SIMULATION AND VALIDATION
-----------------------------
Testing safety logic with its own assumptions proves nothing, so the
simulator (Tests/sim/FlightSafetySimulator.hpp) is independent:
  - LiDAR truth: the beam is rotated by the simulator's OWN Euler matrices
    and marched through the terrain in 2 cm steps with bisection. The
    geometry tests also use the closed-form ray-plane intersection. The
    firmware uses a quaternion and a projection: two different methods
    agreeing to 2e-5 m.
  - Expected results come from TRUTH: the true lift-off time, the true
    touchdown, the true time to contact, not the thresholds.
  - Tuning used seeds 1-3. Defaults were then frozen, and the scenario tests
    use held-out seeds 101-110.
  - D55 runs the REAL preprocessing, Madgwick and UKF before the safety layer.
Honest limits: synthetic motion is not real flight, and the "IMU stillness"
of real pilots is unknown.


Q. WORKED EXAMPLE: ONE LIDAR SAMPLE TO ONE SAFETYEVENT
-------------------------------------------------------
Scenario: started at 30 m over flat ground, 20-degree bank, 5-degree pitch,
heading 30 degrees, sinking 1 m/s then 3.2 m/s from t = 6 s. Real classes,
seed 7.

Step 1 - the sample (t = 9.00 s)
  truth height above ground 14.400 m (sensor)   true slant range 15.383 m
  measured range (noise) r = 15.500 m
  attitude q = (w, x, y, z) = (-0.17172, 0.84827, 0.49849, -0.04954)

Step 2 - beam into ENU (beamEnu)
  d_ENU = q (x) (0,0,1) (x) q* = (-0.2552, 0.2419, -0.9361)
  matches the simulator's independent truth beam to 4 decimals
  projection p = -d_ENU.z = 0.9361  -> tilt = acos(0.9361) = 20.59 deg
  (for a body-down beam cos(tilt) = cos(roll) * cos(pitch) = 0.9397 * 0.9962 = 0.9361:
   the 20-degree bank and 5-degree pitch give 20.59 degrees, not 25)

Step 3 - clearance
  clearance = r * p = 15.500 * 0.9361 = 14.510 m   (truth 14.40)
  sigma = 0.536 m: mostly the 5-degree unverified mounting,
          r sin(theta) sigma = 15.5 * 0.352 * 0.0942 = 0.51 m

Step 4 - closure rate (41 samples over the last 400 ms)
  closure = +2.866 m/s (approaching), sigma 0.708 m/s, latency 200 ms
  (true closure 3.2 m/s: inside 1 sigma)
  UKF vz at that moment: -3.316 m/s (same story from a different sensor)

Step 5 - time to contact
  closure - 2 sigma = 2.866 - 1.416 > 0  -> valid
  TTC = 14.510 / 2.866 = 5.06 s

Step 6 - descent assessment (t = 9.00 s)
  phase Approach (clearance < 30 m since t = 6 s)
  ExcessiveDescent: sink 3.3 < 3.5 m/s caution         -> level 0
  RapidGroundClosure: closure 2.9 < 4 m/s caution      -> level 0
  HazardousApproach: clearance 14.5 < 15 m AND closure 2.9 > 2.5 m/s
                                                       -> desired level Warning
  LowGroundClearance: 14.5 m > 10 m                    -> level 0

Step 7 - warning decision
  The HazardousApproach tracker starts its 500 ms raise persistence at ~8.8 s.

Step 8 - SafetyEvent publication (t = 9.33 s)
  EVENT id 3, episode 1, type HazardousApproach, transition Raised,
        severity Warning, phase Approach,
        vz -3.03 m/s, clearance 13.51 m, closure 3.23 m/s, TTC 4.18 s,
        qualityFlags 0xc68 = ModelPlaceholder | LidarMountingUnverified |
                             FlatTerrainAssumed | ImuMountingUnverified | Unvalidated
  Then at 11.43 s: LowGroundClearance Advisory raised (clearance < 10 m for 1 s).
  No more events while the conditions persist. The snapshot shows them active.

Step 9 - who receives it
  SafetyEventEmitter::emit -> SafetyEventQueue<16> (firmware) -> the future
  audio task pops it and plays a Warning tone once, then repeats from the
  snapshot while active.

S. HOW CRASH DETECTION AND SOS WILL USE THIS
---------------------------------------------
  - The crash detector reads the snapshot and the IMU for impact patterns
    (e.g. a large acceleration spike, then stillness). It publishes
    SafetyEvent{type = CrashSuspected (reserved), source = CrashDetector}
    through the same emitter, so event ids stay one sequence.
  - The emergency manager pops the queue. It decides on SOS using
    CrashSuspected + phase (was Flying, now Landed / Unknown) + time without
    motion + pilot cancel. Severity and episode identity are already there.
  - Audio / LED play Raised / Escalated once and repeat from the snapshot
    while active.
  - Logging registers its own queue with SafetyMonitor::addSink(). Gaps in
    eventId reveal lost events.
Nothing in the producers needs to change for these consumers.


T. QUESTIONS A REVIEWER MAY ASK
--------------------------------
Q: Why not just use altitude to detect landing?
A: Altitude is relative to the launch. You may land 800 m lower. Only its
   CHANGE and STEADINESS are evidence.

Q: What if the LiDAR is mounted crooked?
A: A 10-degree error gives up to 0.85 m error at 10 m (tested). Every output
   says MountingUnverified until it is checked on the real harness.

Q: Could the device think it landed during a long glide in calm air?
A: It needs two ground sources AND no airborne source. With a working LiDAR
   or GNSS, a flying pilot always has one (LidarHigh / out of range,
   GnssFast). With both missing, very calm level flight could look
   "steady + still". This is documented as a limitation.

Q: Why no flare warning?
A: Flare timing depends on the wing, wind, airspeed and technique. One beam
   and a vertical speed cannot determine it safely, so no command is given.

Q: How do you avoid alarm fatigue?
A: Persistence, hysteresis, cooldowns, transition-only events, and
   advisories that never escalate on their own. 20 normal simulated flights
   gave 0 Caution-or-higher.
