===============================================================================
A. HOW PREPROCESSING FITS INTO PARAGUARDIAN
===============================================================================

A.1 Before this milestone
-------------------------
Milestone 1 and the reliability hardening built this chain:

  sensor chip --SPI/I2C/UART--> driver (e.g. Lsm6dsrx)
              --> AcquisitionSource (ImuSource, BaroSource, ...)
              --> AcquisitionScheduler (inside SensorAcquisitionTask, every 10 ms)
              --> SensorDataHub::publish(SensorMessage)
              --> subscribers (queues and mailboxes)

At that point every sample was:
  * in SI units (m/s^2, rad/s, Pa, uT, m),
  * in the CHIP's own axes (whatever way the chip is soldered),
  * stamped with the MCU time at which the read finished, plus a sequence
    number,
  * marked valid/invalid by the driver.

Nothing consumed those samples yet. No calibration existed, nothing compared
the three IMUs, and no barometric altitude was computed.

A.2 What this milestone adds
----------------------------
A middle layer between "raw SI samples" and the future algorithms
(Madgwick, UKF, safety logic):

  raw SensorMessage
    -> 1 validity and timestamp checks       (SampleValidator)
    -> 2 calibration                         (CalibrationController: StationaryDetector,
                                              GyroBiasCalibrator, BaroReferenceCalibrator)
    -> 3 correction and alignment            (gyro - bias; AxisAlignment sensor -> body)
    -> 4 redundant sensor comparison         (RedundancyVoter)
    -> ProcessedSensorFrame                  (one struct with values AND quality metadata)

A.3 Where the code lives, and why it is split into two layers
--------------------------------------------------------------
  Components/Processing/   PORTABLE C++17. No HAL, no FreeRTOS, no heap.
                           The same .cpp files compile into the firmware AND
                           into the laptop test program.
  Components/Tasks/SensorProcessingTask.*
                           The ONLY new file that knows about FreeRTOS. It
                           takes messages from a queue, calls the portable
                           pipeline, and publishes frames.

Why this split: about 95 % of the logic can then be tested in milliseconds
on a laptop with sanitizers, deterministic simulated data and exact expected
values. On the target you could only test it slowly, by hand, once hardware
exists. The pattern is the same as the drivers (portable, with injected bus
interfaces) and the acquisition scheduler.

A.4 Main design rules followed everywhere
-----------------------------------------
  1. Missing data is NEVER zero. Every value has a valid flag. A frame can
     say "no angular rate this time", and that is better than reporting 0
     rad/s.
  2. Every sensor keeps its own timestamp. Nothing is shifted, interpolated
     or invented.
  3. No dynamic memory. Every object has a fixed size known at compile time.
     The pipeline is a member of the task object, which is a static
     singleton.
  4. Every loop has a compile-time bound (3 IMUs, 2 barometers, 8 segments,
     23 reason bits, queue depth 64).
  5. The acquisition task is never slowed down. Its only extra cost is
     copying 88 bytes into one more queue per message.
  6. Reuse existing types: Vec3f, SensorId, SampleHeader, ImuSample,
     SensorMessage, SensorDataHub, RtosSampleQueue, ICriticalSection and
     SequenceTracker (extended, not replaced).


===============================================================================
B. SENSOR VALIDITY AND FRESHNESS
===============================================================================

Files: Components/Processing/SampleValidator.hpp / .cpp
       Components/Processing/ProcessingTypes.hpp       (reject:: reason bits)
       Components/Processing/ProcessingConfig.hpp      (ValidationConfig)
       Components/Acquisition/SampleFreshness.hpp      (SequenceTracker, extended)

B.1 Purpose
-----------
Decide, for ONE incoming sample, whether it may be used, and if not, record
exactly why. The validator does not compare sensors with each other. It
checks each sample on its own merits and against that same sensor's history
(sequence numbers and timestamps).

B.2 Inputs and outputs
----------------------
  Input : const SensorMessage& msg   - the tagged union published by acquisition
          uint32_t nowMs              - current MCU time (processing task)
  Output: ValidationResult (SampleValidator.hpp):
            reasons      bit mask of reject:: values (0 = nothing wrong)
            ageMs        nowMs - timestampMs (0 if the timestamp is in the future)
            sequenceGap  how many samples of this sensor were never received
            sequence     First / InOrder / Gap / Duplicate / OutOfOrder / Invalid
            accepted     no "whole-sample" reason is set
            accelUsable  IMU only: accepted AND no accelerometer-channel reason
            gyroUsable   IMU only: accepted AND no gyro-channel reason

Why a bit mask instead of one boolean (the brief asked for this
explicitly): one sample can be wrong in several ways at once, for example
stale AND saturated. A boolean would hide which. With bits, the debugger
shows e.g. reasons = 0x0810, which decodes to Stale (bit 4) plus
GyroSaturated (bit 11).

Why separate accelUsable/gyroUsable: the LSM6DSRX accelerometer and gyro
saturate independently. During a fast spin the gyro may hit +-2000 dps while
the accelerometer is perfectly fine. Rejecting the whole sample would throw
away good data.

B.3 The checks, step by step (SampleValidator.cpp)
--------------------------------------------------
contentReasons() - line 47 - looks only at the sample's contents:
  * No sample at all (type None)                         -> NoSample
  * id does not match the message type (an IMU payload
    claiming to be Baro0), via idMatchesType() line 34    -> UnknownSensor
  * driver said header.valid = false                     -> FlagInvalid
  * IMU:  NaN/Inf in accel, gyro or temperature          -> NonFinite
          (the range checks are skipped then: comparing NaN is meaningless)
          accelSaturated / gyroSaturated from the driver -> Accel/GyroSaturated
          any component above the configured full scale  -> Accel/GyroOutOfRange
          status register said "no new data"             -> Accel/GyroNotNew
          die temperature outside -40..105 C              -> TemperatureRange
  * Barometer: NaN, pressure <= 0 or outside 300..1250 hPa,
          temperature outside -40..85 C
  * Magnetometer: NaN, saturated, |component| > 800 uT
  * GNSS: NaN in a used float, no valid fix, negative accuracy estimate
  * LiDAR: NaN, out of range or negative, weak signal

validate() - line 134 - adds the time and sequence checks:
  line 152  ahead = diffMs(timestamp, now). A timestamp more than maxFutureMs
            (2 ms) AHEAD of "now" is impossible -> TimestampFuture.
            (1-2 ms ahead is tolerated: HAL_GetTick() has 1 ms steps and the
            two reads can straddle a tick.)
  line 156  ageMs = ageMs(timestamp, now). If age > maxAgeMs[sensor] -> Stale.
  line 165  SequenceTracker classification: Duplicate (the same sequence
            number again) or OutOfOrder (older than one already seen).
  line 169  "newer sequence number but OLDER timestamp than the last accepted
            sample" -> TimestampBackwards. Time must not run backwards for a
            sensor; if it does, the clock or the driver is inconsistent.
  line 175  accepted = no whole-sample reason set.
  line 181  an IMU sample with NEITHER channel usable is rejected as a whole.

record() keeps per-sensor accepted/rejected counts, the last reason mask,
and one counter per reason bit (all saturating: they stop at UINT32_MAX
rather than wrapping to 0).

B.4 The deliberate NON-check: free fall
---------------------------------------
There is no minimum on |acceleration|. An accelerometer measures SPECIFIC
FORCE (acceleration minus gravity). In free fall it reads about 0, and in a
paragliding collapse or a fall that is exactly what you want to detect
later, not throw away. A dead IMU is detected in other ways: the driver
flags errors, it stops producing data (freshness), or it disagrees with the
other IMUs (redundancy). Test: prep_validation_free_fall_is_not_a_fault.

B.5 Freshness thresholds (per sensor, ValidationConfig::maxAgeMs)
-----------------------------------------------------------------
  IMU 30 ms   (period 10 ms x 3)       Baro 60 ms (period 20 ms x 3)
  Mag 60 ms   (period 20 ms x 3)       GNSS 2000 ms (= the NeoM9n driver's stale timeout)
  LiDAR 50 ms (frames about every 10 ms; the driver's own stale limit is 100 ms)
Reason for "about 3 periods": allow one missed acquisition cycle plus tick
jitter and queue latency, and still reject anything older. One global
threshold would be wrong. 30 ms is far too short for GNSS, and 2 s is far
too long for an IMU.

The limits are applied TWICE:
  * at ingest, by the validator (a sample that waited too long in the queue);
  * at frame time, by PreprocessingPipeline::fresh() (line 75): the newest
    stored sample of a sensor that has since gone silent becomes "not
    fresh" and stops contributing.

B.6 Sequence tracking (SampleFreshness.hpp, extended)
-----------------------------------------------------
Every driver increments header.sequence once per sample it produces, and it
does not reset on re-init. I checked this in Lsm6dsrx.cpp:223,
Bmp585.cpp:186 and Mmc5983ma.cpp:189. So for one sensor:
  expected = last + 1
  sequence == expected            -> InOrder
  sequence  > expected (signed)   -> Gap: (sequence - expected) samples were lost
  sequence == last                -> Duplicate
  sequence  < last                -> OutOfOrder
observeHeader() (line 80) returns this classification. The older observe()
keeps its exact original behaviour (it returns only the gap), so the 120
existing tests and HealthMonitor are unaffected. The comparison uses
int32 differences, so it also survives the sequence counter wrapping.

Numerical example (prep_validation_duplicates_out_of_order_and_gaps):
  seq 10 at t=100 -> First, accepted
  seq 10 again    -> Duplicate, rejected
  seq 14 at t=140 -> Gap = 3 (11, 12 and 13 never arrived), accepted
  seq 12 at t=120 -> OutOfOrder, rejected;  lostCount(Imu0) = 3

B.7 Memory, timing, errors, tests
---------------------------------
  Memory: SampleValidator = 548 B (8 sensors x 24 B state + 23 counters +
          the SequenceTracker). It is a member of the pipeline; lifetime = forever.
  Timing: O(1); a few dozen comparisons and up to 7 isfinite() calls.
  Errors: never throws, never asserts. It reports through the result.
  Tests : 15 in Tests/unit/test_preprocessing_validation.cpp (invalid
          flags, NaN/Inf, saturation per channel, range, not-new, free
          fall, per-sensor staleness, future/backwards timestamps,
          duplicates, gaps, out-of-order, id mismatch, wraparound).


===============================================================================
C. TIMESTAMP SYNCHRONIZATION
===============================================================================

File: Components/Processing/TimeBase.hpp

C.1 The convention (what a timestamp MEANS)
-------------------------------------------
SampleHeader::timestampMs = HAL_GetTick() milliseconds at the moment the
driver FINISHED reading the sample. That is NOT the physical instant the
quantity was measured:
  * an IMU's digital filter delays the signal,
  * a BMP585 conversion takes a few ms,
  * a GNSS solution refers to an epoch that may be 10s of ms earlier.
These latencies are not known for this hardware. The code therefore states
the convention and provides a hook to correct it later
(IMeasurementTimeModel, FixedLatencyTimeModel, with all latencies set to 0).

C.2 Two samples in the same cycle are NOT simultaneous
------------------------------------------------------
The three IMUs are read one after another within a cycle (and the scheduler
rotates the order), so their timestamps can differ by a millisecond or two.
Barometers at 50 Hz may even be read in different 10 ms cycles. The code
never assumes equality. The redundancy voter compares two samples only if
their timestamps are within maxSkewMs (IMU 15 ms, barometer 30 ms). Its
output carries BOTH the newest contributing timestamp and the spread
(newest - oldest), so a consumer knows how "smeared" a fused value is.

C.3 Wraparound
--------------
A uint32 millisecond counter wraps after 2^32 ms = 49.7 days. The trick:

  diffMs(later, earlier) = (int32_t)(later - earlier)

Unsigned subtraction wraps modulo 2^32, and interpreting the result as
signed gives the correct signed interval whenever the true interval is
below 2^31 ms (24.8 days).
Example: earlier = 0xFFFFFFF0 (16 ms before the wrap), later = 0x00000010
(16 ms after it). later - earlier = 0x00000020 = 32, which is correct. The
reverse gives -32.
ageMs() returns 0 for a timestamp in the future instead of a huge unsigned
number. That matters: without it, a future timestamp would look about 49
days old.
Every interval compared in preprocessing is seconds at most, so the 24.8-day
limit is never approached.
Tests: prep_time_wraparound_diff_and_age, prep_time_validator_across_wrap,
and prep_e2e_runs_across_timestamp_wraparound. The last one runs a complete
calibration that crosses the wrap 3 s in, and checks that nothing is
rejected and dt stays 10 ms.

C.4 Per-sensor latest valid measurement and its age
---------------------------------------------------
PreprocessingPipeline keeps one small "state" struct per sensor (ImuState,
BaroState, MagState, GnssState, LidarState). Each holds the newest ACCEPTED
sample (already corrected and aligned) together with ITS OWN timestamp. When
a frame is built, every channel reports:
  timestampMs  (its own),
  ageMs        (frame time - timestampMs),
  fresh        (ageMs <= that sensor's limit).
Nothing is copied forward as if it were new: an old value stays labelled
with its old time.

C.5 Out-of-order arrival
------------------------
Per sensor: rejected (OutOfOrder / TimestampBackwards). Across sensors,
arrival order does not matter, because each sensor has its own slot. With
one FIFO queue and one producer, per-sensor reordering should never happen
on the real system. The checks protect against driver bugs and future
multi-producer designs.

C.6 GNSS time vs MCU time
-------------------------
A GNSS sample carries two times:
  gnssTimeOfWeek_ms   receiver clock (GPS time of week)
  header.timestampMs  MCU clock when the UBX message was parsed
They are different clocks with an unknown offset and drift. The frame passes
both through (gnss.timeOfWeek_ms, gnss.timestampMs) and never converts one
into the other. ProcessedSensorFrame::timeDomain documents that every
*timestampMs field is MCU time. A real mapping needs the receiver's
TIMEPULSE signal on a timer-capture pin, which is a hardware decision (see
HARDWARE_INTEGRATION_TODO.md section 12).

C.7 No interpolation - why
--------------------------
Interpolating between two IMU samples to "align" them assumes the signal is
smooth between samples, and it adds half a period of latency. Neither has
been justified for this hardware. Both are easy to add later behind
IMeasurementTimeModel, with their own tests. The brief forbade adding them
without documented assumptions and tests.

C.8 Timestamp jitter
--------------------
prep_e2e_timestamp_jitter_tolerated_and_dt_follows_samples makes every IMU
timestamp randomly 0-4 ms early. The result: nothing is rejected, every
frame is Healthy, and gyroDtMs varies between 6 and 14 ms, following the
real timestamps. The time step a Madgwick filter will use is MEASURED, not
assumed to be 10 ms.

D. STATIONARY DETECTION
===============================================================================

Files: Components/Processing/StationaryDetector.hpp / .cpp
       Components/Processing/RunningStats.hpp   (streaming statistics)
       ProcessingConfig.hpp: StationaryConfig

D.1 Purpose
-----------
Answer "has THIS IMU been continuously still for a whole observation
window?" Gyro-bias calibration is only valid if the true rotation rate is
zero, and the barometer reference is only meaningful if the device is not
being carried up or down. One detector exists per IMU.

D.2 Input, output, status values
--------------------------------
  add(timestampMs, gyro_radps, accel_mps2) -> StationaryStatus
    InsufficientData  window not yet long enough, or too few samples
    Moving            this sample, or the window statistics, showed motion
    Stationary        a full, continuous, quiet window was observed
  report() gives the evidence: samples, elapsed time, means, standard
  deviations, segment spreads, the cause of the last restart, and restart
  counters.
Only samples the validator accepted with BOTH channels usable are fed in, in
SENSOR axes. Stillness does not depend on how the chip is mounted.

D.3 The tests a window must pass (StationaryDetector.cpp)
---------------------------------------------------------
1) INSTANTANEOUS, on every sample (line 118 / line 122):
     |w| <= 0.10 rad/s           and        | |a| - 9.80665 | <= 0.6 m/s^2
   In words: no obvious rotation, and the accelerometer senses roughly
   gravity only. The gyro limit is deliberately LOOSE because the gyro is
   not calibrated yet. Its bias (typically of the order of 1 dps = 0.017
   rad/s for MEMS gyros; confirm with the LSM6DSRX datasheet) must not
   trigger "motion".
   Any violation calls motion(): status Moving, the window restarts at the
   next sample.

2) NO GAPS: if two consecutive samples are more than 50 ms apart, the window
   restarts (cause DataGap). We cannot vouch for a period we did not observe.

3) LONG ENOUGH (line 151): elapsed = last - first >= 5000 ms AND samples >=
   minSamples(). minSamples = (5000 / 10) x 0.8 = 400. The brief warned that
   5 s at 100 Hz is "about 500 samples, but missing data must be accounted
   for". This allows 20 % loss. With MORE loss, the window simply stretches
   until 400 samples have been seen (prep_stationary_intermittent_missing_data:
   every 4th sample missing -> Stationary only after 5 s).

4) QUIET STATISTICS, once the window is long enough
   (windowStatisticsQuiet(), line 50):
     per-axis gyro standard deviation   <= 0.01 rad/s
     standard deviation of |a|          <= 0.05 m/s^2
     spread of 1-second segment means:  gyro per axis <= 0.005 rad/s
                                        accel PER AXIS <= 0.05 m/s^2
   A constant bias does not raise a standard deviation, but motion does.
   That is why the gyro test is on the SPREAD, not on the mean.

D.4 Why the per-axis accelerometer drift test exists (a defect found during this milestone)
------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------
Imagine the wearer turning slowly at a constant 0.05 rad/s (2.9 deg/s):
  * every gyro sample reads 0.05: below the 0.10 limit;
  * the gyro standard deviation is about 0: it looks EXACTLY like a bias;
  * |a| stays exactly g, because rotating does not change gravity's size.
The first version of the detector accepted this as "still". The gyro
calibrator would then have stored the rotation as a 2.9 dps "bias", and
every later angular rate would have been wrong by that amount.
What does change is gravity's DIRECTION in the chip's axes. After 5 s at
0.05 rad/s the accelerometer y-component moves by 9.81 x sin(0.25) = about
2.4 m/s^2. Even at 0.005 rad/s it moves by 0.245 m/s^2. The five 1-second
segment means are then 0.025, 0.074, 0.123, 0.172, 0.221, a spread of 0.196
m/s^2, against a limit of 0.05. The window is rejected with cause
AccelDrift. Test: prep_stationary_slow_constant_rotation_not_mistaken_for_bias,
for both rates.

D.5 Why no 500-sample history buffer
------------------------------------
All the tests above need only:
  * a running mean and variance (Welford, below): 3 numbers per quantity;
  * the sum and count of each 1-second segment: 8 slots per axis.
So the detector costs about 0.7 KB instead of 500 x 24 B = 12 KB of raw
samples per IMU.

D.6 Welford's algorithm (RunningStats.hpp, lines 49-54)
-------------------------------------------------------
For every new value x:
  n    = n + 1
  d    = x - mean
  mean = mean + d / n                 (line 52)
  M2   = M2 + d * (x - mean)          (line 53; the NEW mean)
  sample variance = M2 / (n - 1)
Worked example: the values 0.010, 0.012, 0.008
  n=1: d=0.010  mean=0.010  M2=0
  n=2: d=0.002  mean=0.011  M2 = 0 + 0.002 x 0.001 = 0.000002
  n=3: d=-0.003 mean=0.010  M2 = 0.000002 + (-0.003) x (-0.002) = 0.000008
  variance = 0.000008 / 2 = 0.000004   -> std = 0.002
  (Check: the deviations from 0.010 are 0, +0.002 and -0.002; their squares
  sum to 8e-6, and 8e-6 / 2 = 4e-6. It matches.)
Why not "mean of squares minus square of mean"? For a barometer, x is about
101325 and the spread is about 1. Squaring gives about 1.03e10, and float
has only about 7 significant digits, so the difference would be pure
rounding noise. Welford works with deviations only. The accumulators are
double. The STM32H743 FPU (fpv5-d16, confirmed in .cproject) does double
precision in hardware.

D.7 Memory, timing, errors, tests
---------------------------------
  Memory: about 0.7 KB per detector, 3 detectors, inside CalibrationController.
  Timing: O(1) per sample: 2 norms (sqrt), 4 Welford updates and 6
          segment adds. Once the window is long enough, the statistics are
          re-evaluated per sample (6 spreads over 8 slots each).
  Errors: non-finite input is ignored. A sample not newer than the previous
          one is ignored.
  Tests : 9 prep_stationary_* tests: perfectly still; realistic noise with
          uncalibrated bias; constant rotation; slow constant rotation (two
          rates); translational push (both a gentle 1.5 m/s^2, caught by
          the window statistics, and a 3 m/s^2 shove, caught instantly);
          short spike (needs a whole new 5 s); missing data and gaps; slow
          drift accepted vs fast drift rejected; noisy (body sway) window.


===============================================================================
E. GYROSCOPE CALIBRATION
===============================================================================

Files: Components/Processing/Calibration.hpp / .cpp (GyroBiasCalibrator, AccelCalibration)
       Components/Processing/CalibrationController.hpp / .cpp (the session)

E.1 The physics in one paragraph
--------------------------------
A MEMS gyro reading is  w_measured = w_true + b + noise. When the device is
still, w_true = 0. Earth's rotation is 7.3e-5 rad/s, below the noise, and is
ignored. So the AVERAGE reading over a still window IS the bias b:
  b_hat = mean(w_measured)              calibrated w = w_measured - b_hat
The more samples, the better the estimate. The uncertainty of a mean is the
noise divided by sqrt(n) (the "standard error"):
  sigma_b = sigma / sqrt(n)
Example: sigma = 0.0015 rad/s, n = 500 -> sigma_b = 6.7e-5 rad/s = 0.004 dps.

E.2 GyroBiasCalibrator (Calibration.cpp)
----------------------------------------
  begin()     starts collecting. The ACTIVE (previous) calibration stays in
              use meanwhile.
  restart()   throws away the samples collected so far (after motion).
  add(gyro)   Welford update per axis (RunningStats3).
  finish(now, sessionId), line 29, evaluates the candidate:
     n < 400 samples                       -> fail InsufficientSamples
     mean or std not finite                -> fail InvalidValue
     max per-axis std > 0.01 rad/s         -> fail TooNoisy
     |mean| on any axis > 10 dps           -> fail InvalidValue (that is
                                              motion or a broken chip, not a bias)
     otherwise (lines 58-61) store: bias = mean, noiseStd = std,
     biasStdError = std / sqrt(n), samples, timestamp, sessionId, valid = true.
  correct(w)  returns w - bias if valid, otherwise w unchanged. (The pipeline
              then marks the gyro as uncalibrated, see I.)
  state()     Collecting / Valid / Failed / NotCalibrated.

FAILURE POLICY: if a later calibration fails, the PREVIOUS valid bias is
KEPT, and lastFailure() records why. Reason: a bias is a property of the
chip. Last session's estimate is still a better correction than none.
(Barometers do the opposite, see G.4. The two are different on purpose.)

E.3 The session: CalibrationController (one state machine for all sensors)
-------------------------------------------------------------------------
  request(now) (line 24): sessionId++, state Collecting; every detector
      reset, every gyro calibrator begin(), every barometer calibrator begin(now).
  onImu(i, t, gyro, accel) (line 57): called by the pipeline for every
      accepted IMU sample with both channels usable, in RAW sensor axes.
        - the detector for IMU i gets the sample;
        - line 64: if it says Moving -> restartAll(): EVERY detector, gyro
          accumulator and barometer accumulator restarts. The device is one
          rigid body: if one IMU moved, everything moved;
        - line 71: if the detector just (re)started its own window (its
          first sample, or after a data gap), the gyro accumulator is reset
          too, so the two always cover the same samples;
        - otherwise the gyro calibrator gets the sample.
  onBaro(j, t, p) (line 77): barometer samples older than the current window
      start (line 83) are ignored. They belong to a period with motion.
  poll(now) (line 89): called after every message.
        - session older than 60 s (line 94) -> failSession(TimedOut), or
          NoStationaryImu if no IMU sample was ever seen;
        - complete when at least ONE detector is Stationary AND (every IMU
          seen in this session is Stationary OR the window is older than
          5 s + 1 s grace) (line 114). The grace stops a missing or
          intermittent IMU from blocking the other two forever.
  complete(): finishes each calibrator individually. An IMU whose detector
      is not Stationary is finished with zero samples, so it fails
      InsufficientSamples and keeps any previous result.
  failSession(): keeps every previous result. With retryOnFailure (the
      default) a new session starts immediately, and the failure is counted
      and remembered.

KEY RULE: nothing is calibrated without INERTIAL confirmation of stillness.
The brief warned that "altitude not changing" does not mean "still". Test
prep_session_steady_barometer_alone_never_calibrates: with no IMUs, the
session ends with NoStationaryImu and the barometers stay uncalibrated.

E.4 Worked example from the tests
---------------------------------
prep_gyrocal_known_offsets_with_noise_error_metric:
  true bias (0.0123, -0.0311, 0.0047) rad/s = (+0.70, -1.78, +0.27) dps
  noise sigma = 0.0015 rad/s per axis, 500 samples
  result: |bias_hat - bias| = 1.99e-4 rad/s (0.011 dps)
          reported standard error 6.5e-5 rad/s per axis (theory 6.7e-5)
  The 3-axis error of 1.99e-4 is about 1.7 x sqrt(3) x 6.7e-5, which is
  normal for a three-axis random error.
prep_session_three_imus_different_signed_biases: three IMUs with different
positive and negative biases through the whole session -> worst error
1.34e-4 rad/s, completed exactly at 5.0 s.

E.5 Why the ACCELEROMETER is not calibrated (single-pose limitation)
---------------------------------------------------------------------
At rest, the accelerometer reads  a = -g_sensor + b , where g_sensor is
gravity expressed in the chip's axes and b is the offset.
  * We know |g_sensor| = 9.81, but not its DIRECTION, because the
    orientation is unknown.
  * That is 3 unknown offsets + 2 unknown direction angles = 5 unknowns,
    against 3 measured numbers. The system cannot be solved.
  * The tempting shortcut, "offset = mean - (0, 0, -9.81)", silently ASSUMES
    the board is perfectly level with Z vertical. Any tilt would be
    "calibrated" into a fake offset (1 deg of tilt = 0.17 m/s^2 of error),
    and every later reading would be wrong.
What IS possible later: a multi-orientation procedure (e.g. place the module
on each of its 6 faces). Each pose gives |a| = g with a different unknown
direction, and together they determine offset and scale.
AccelCalibration (Calibration.hpp) is the interface for that:
  calibrated = scaleMisalignment x (measured - bias), valid = false by default.
applyAccelCalibration() returns the measurement unchanged while valid is
false. The pipeline applies it per IMU (setAccelCalibration()), and every
frame carries quality::AccelNotCalibrated until all three are valid. The
driver's factory sensitivity (mg/LSB) is still used. No constant was
invented.

E.6 Memory, timing, tests
-------------------------
  GyroBiasCalibrator about 150 B, x3. CalibrationController total 3 408 B
  (host sizeof; mostly the three detectors).
  Timing: O(1) per sample; finish() runs once per session.
  Tests (session and gyro): known offsets with error metric; positive and
  negative biases on 3 IMUs; noise; interrupted by motion; different

F. COORDINATE ALIGNMENT
===============================================================================

Files: Components/Processing/AxisAlignment.hpp / .cpp
       Components/Processing/VectorMath.hpp (Mat3f, rotationX/Y/Z)

F.1 The frames
--------------
SENSOR frame: the chip's own X/Y/Z, as printed in its datasheet. This is
what the drivers publish.

BODY frame, chosen: FRD, right-handed
  +X forward (the direction the pilot faces)
  +Y right
  +Z down
At rest and level the accelerometer reads (0, 0, -9.81). It points UP,
because it measures the force holding the device up against gravity.
Why FRD: it is the aerospace convention that pairs with NED
(North-East-Down). The standard attitude matrix R_nb (body -> NED) is then
directly what Madgwick-type filters produce.

NAVIGATION frame: the project expects ENU (East-North-Up). The conversion is
two explicit steps, and they are never mixed silently:
  v_NED = R_nb x v_body                    (R_nb: future attitude filter)
  v_ENU = C x v_NED,  C = kEnuFromNed = | 0 1  0 |
                                        | 1 0  0 |
                                        | 0 0 -1 |
  i.e. east = NED y, north = NED x, up = -NED z.
C is its own inverse and has determinant +1. Swapping two axes (-1) and
negating one (-1) give +1 together: it is the 180 deg rotation about the
line halfway between North and East. (An earlier comment in the code
claimed -1. The test caught it, and the comment was fixed.)
Example: NED velocity (3 north, 4 east, 1 down) -> ENU (4, 3, -1).

F.2 Mounting matrices
---------------------
For each IMU and the magnetometer:  v_body = R x v_sensor, where
R = R_body_from_sensor.
The SAME R is applied to an IMU's accelerometer AND its gyro (both are on
one die and share one axis triad). See PreprocessingPipeline.cpp lines
158-159.
Example: chip rotated +90 deg about Z,  R = rotationZ(90 deg) =
  | 0 -1 0 |
  | 1  0 0 |   sensor (1,0,0) -> body (0,1,0): the chip's X points to body right.
  | 0  0 1 |

F.3 What makes a matrix acceptable (isProperRotation, lines 26-44)
-------------------------------------------------------------------
  1. all 9 entries finite;
  2. R x R^T = I within 1e-4 per entry (line 35). The columns are
     perpendicular unit vectors, which is exactly "preserves lengths and
     angles";
  3. det(R) = +1 within 1e-4 (line 44). No mirror image.
Why reject det = -1: flipping ONE axis turns a right-handed frame into a
left-handed one. No way of soldering a chip can do that, so such a matrix
means a typo in the configuration. Flipping TWO axes (a chip on the
underside of the board = 180 deg about X: y -> -y, z -> -z) is a real
rotation and is accepted.
setMounting() (line 63) rejects bad matrices (and sensors with no vector
output, such as barometers), KEEPS the previous mounting, and counts the
rejection.
fromAxisMap() (line 47) builds R from a table like "body X = +sensor Y,
body Y = -sensor X, body Z = +sensor Z", which is the usual way to turn a
PCB drawing into a matrix. Line 54 puts the sign at row k, column `axis`.
No Euler-angle function is offered. The order of Euler angles (XYZ vs ZYX,
intrinsic vs extrinsic) is a classic source of silent errors. To compose
rotations, write the product explicitly: R = Rz x Ry x Rx means "about X
first, then Y, then Z, all about fixed axes".

F.4 The placeholder - stated plainly
------------------------------------
The constructor (line 13) sets every mounting to the IDENTITY with
verified = false, because the PCB orientation is UNKNOWN. Every frame
carries quality::MountingUnverified until real values are installed with
verified = true. Nothing in the code claims to know the board axes.
Hardware procedure: HARDWARE_INTEGRATION_TODO.md section 12.

F.5 Tests (9)

(compared with rotationX(pi)); single-axis flip rejected; permutations
(including a repeated axis and a zero sign, both rejected); magnitude

G. BAROMETRIC REFERENCE CALIBRATION
===============================================================================

Files: Components/Processing/Calibration.hpp / .cpp (BaroReferenceCalibrator, baro::)

G.1 Purpose
-----------
During the same still window as the gyro calibration, each BMP585
independently estimates its reference pressure P0, the pressure "here, now".
Afterwards each barometer reports height RELATIVE to that point.

G.2 BaroReferenceCalibrator
---------------------------
  begin(start) / restart(start): reset the statistics and the 1 s segments.
  add(t, p): ignores NaN and p <= 0; Welford update plus segment means.
  finish(now, sessionId), line 104:
     line 111: the reference is set INVALID first; it only becomes valid again on success
     n < 200 (5 s at 50 Hz = 250 expected, 80 %)        -> InsufficientSamples
     mean not finite or <= 0                            -> InvalidValue
     std > 10 Pa  (about 0.85 m)                        -> TooNoisy
     spread of 1 s segment means > 12 Pa (about 1 m),
       line 129                                         -> Drifting
     otherwise store P0 = mean (double), std, spread, samples, time,
     sessionId and scale height (G.5).

G.3 The formula (specified by the brief)
----------------------------------------
  h_rel = 44330 x [ 1 - (P / P0)^0.1903 ]          (Calibration.cpp line 172)
In words: in the standard atmosphere, pressure falls with height along a
known curve. 0.1903 = R x L / (g x M) (gas constant x temperature lapse
rate / (gravity x molar mass of air)), and 44330 m = T0 / L = 288.15 K /
0.0065 K/m.
Worked example: P0 = 101325 Pa, P = 101213 Pa (112 Pa lower)
  P/P0 = 0.998895;  ^0.1903 -> 0.999790;  1 - that = 0.000210;  x 44330 = 9.33 m
Rule of thumb: near sea level, 1 m is about 12 Pa, and 1 hPa is about 8.3 m.
Double precision is used: P/P0 is very close to 1, and the result is a
small difference of numbers close to 1.
pow() runs only in the processing task (2 barometers x 50 Hz = 100 calls/s),
never in the acquisition task or the driver.

G.4 Independent references, same session, and why a failure INVALIDATES
------------------------------------------------------------------------
Each barometer has its own P0, so a constant offset between the two chips
(manufacturing tolerance) cancels: each reads 0 m at the calibration point.
Test prep_session_two_baros_independent_references_with_offset: a 35 Pa
offset (about 3 m in absolute terms) gives P0 values 35 Pa apart, and both
relative altitudes agree.
Both references carry the sessionId. The pipeline compares only barometers
whose references come from the SAME (newest) session
(PreprocessingPipeline.cpp line 289). Their zeros then refer to one place
and time.
If a new session fails for one barometer, that barometer's reference becomes
INVALID instead of keeping the old one. Otherwise barometer 0 could be
zeroed at the launch site and barometer 1 still at the car park: they would
"disagree" by the height difference and confuse the voter. This is
"do not silently switch reference frames", enforced in code.
A session that times out (no result at all) keeps the previous, mutually
consistent references.

G.5 A finding about the specified formula (please read for the review)
----------------------------------------------------------------------
44330 m uses the SEA-LEVEL temperature (288.15 K). Relative to a reference
at altitude h0, the formula overstates height differences by the factor
44330 / (44330 - h0):
  take-off at 1500 m, true climb 300 m  ->  formula gives 310.5 m (+3.5 %).
Test prep_baro_formula_high_takeoff_scale_error_and_isa_option computes the
expected value independently and confirms this.
The code offers BaroCalibrationConfig::altitudeModel:
  StandardSeaLevelScale  (DEFAULT, the specified formula)
  IsaAtReference         scale = 44330 x (P0/101325)^0.1903 = ISA temperature at P0 / L
                         -> exactly 300.0 m in the same test.
Both still ignore the REAL air temperature. A day 20 K warmer than ISA
changes the scale by about 7 %, which is more than the difference between
the two models. That is why the default was left as specified, and why the
choice is recorded as a hardware/flight-test decision.

G.6 Weather - a limitation that preprocessing cannot remove
-----------------------------------------------------------
Weather changes pressure by about 1 hPa over hours. With the device sitting
still, that reads as 8.3 m of "climb" (prep_e2e_slow_weather_drift_appears_as_altitude_change).
Preprocessing cannot tell weather from height. A future estimator must
(GNSS altitude, the LiDAR near the ground, a drift state in the UKF). The
frame's barometric altitude is therefore described as a BASELINE relative
estimate, not a navigation solution.

G.7 Tests (barometer-related, 11)
---------------------------------
reference -> 0 m and known points via the INVERSE formula; the high-site
scale error and the ISA option; zero / negative / NaN pressure; mean, count
and variance; too noisy; carried up 2 m in 5 s (Drifting) vs weather-rate
drift (accepted); insufficient samples; offset between barometers; one
barometer missing; samples before a motion discarded.
End to end: a 100 m climb at 5 m/s is tracked with a worst error of 0.22 m.

H. REDUNDANT SENSOR VALIDATION
===============================================================================

Files: Components/Processing/RedundancyVoter.hpp / .cpp

H.1 Purpose
-----------
Three IMUs and two barometers measure the same physical quantities. The
voter decides:
  * whether they agree;
  * which ones to use;
  * whether a faulty one can be identified;
  * when to stop trusting a sensor, and when to trust it again.
One voter instance is used per quantity: accelerometer (3), gyro (3),
barometric altitude (2, passed as the vector {h, 0, 0}). The brief asked
for acceleration and angular rate to be compared separately, and a sensor
can fail in one channel only.

H.2 Inputs / outputs
--------------------
  update(VoteInput in[3], bool newData) -> VoteResult
    VoteInput: available (fresh, valid, calibrated, aligned), value, timestamp
    VoteResult: status, valid, value, timestamp (newest) and oldest,
                candidate/contributing/excluded/outlier/skewed masks,
                partialAgreement, maxPairDifference
Masks are bit i = sensor i, so 0x5 = IMU0 and IMU2. The frame copies them,
so "exactly which sensors contributed" is always reported.

H.3 One update, step by step (RedundancyVoter.cpp)
--------------------------------------------------
1. Candidates. A sample more than maxSkewMs older than the newest candidate
   (line 63) is not compared (skewedMask): it describes another instant.
2. Agreement (line 94, agree() at line 30):
      |v_i - v_j| <= absTolerance + relTolerance x max(|v_i|, |v_j|)
   The relative part matters during large signals. At 4 g, two good IMUs
   with 1 % sensitivity error differ by about 0.4 m/s^2 just from scale.
3. Voters = candidates not EXCLUDED. With 3 voters, sensor i is this
   update's OUTLIER if it disagrees with BOTH others WHILE those two agree
   (line 112). That is majority evidence.
4. Output table:
     3 contributors, all 3 pairs agree    -> mean,              Healthy
     3 contributors, 2 of 3 pairs agree   -> component median,  Degraded (partialAgreement)
        (a "chain": A~B and B~C, but A!~C, so nobody is clearly wrong)
     3 contributors, no pair agrees       -> no output,         Ambiguous
     2 contributors agree                 -> mean,              Healthy only if 2 is ALL
                                                                configured sensors (barometers);
                                                                Degraded for IMUs
     2 contributors disagree              -> no output,         Ambiguous (nobody blamed)
     1 contributor                        -> that one,          Degraded (cannot be cross-checked)
        ... unless a sensor that is RE-QUALIFYING disagrees with it (line 139):
            that is a 1-vs-1 conflict, so no side is picked -> Ambiguous
     0 candidates                         -> no output,         Unavailable
5. Persistence and hysteresis (only when newData is true, so the same
   sample is never counted twice):
     outlier 5 times IN A ROW            -> EXCLUDED (line 196)
     excluded but agrees with the output
     20 times in a row                    -> re-included (line 210)
     absent for 10 updates               -> must RE-QUALIFY on return
                                            (20 agreements) before contributing (line 76)
   For barometers: 3 / 10 / 5 (they update at 50 Hz).
   A single bad sample is left out of THAT sample's value (it is obviously
   wrong right now), but it never changes the SELECTION (the excluded set).
   That is how "avoid changing selection on a single noisy sample" is met
   without averaging in a value we already know is wrong.

H.4 Worked example (IMU accelerometer x-components, z = -9.81 on all)
---------------------------------------------------------------------
  IMU0 0.10   IMU1 0.15   IMU2 3.20  (m/s^2)
  tolerance for pairs with IMU2 = 1.0 + 0.05 x 10.32 = 1.52
  |0-1| = 0.05 agree;  |0-2| = 3.10 disagree;  |1-2| = 3.05 disagree
  -> IMU2 is the outlier; output = mean(IMU0, IMU1) = 0.125; status Degraded;
     contributingMask 0x3, outlierMask 0x4.
  If this continues for 5 consecutive new samples, excludedMask becomes 0x4.
  If IMU2 then agrees for 20 consecutive samples, it is re-included and
  the status returns to Healthy.
End-to-end (prep_e2e_biased_imu_detected_excluded_output_unaffected): a
0.3 rad/s (17 dps) gyro fault on IMU1 is excluded after 5 frames (50 ms).
The fused output never moved more than 0.005 rad/s from the truth. The
accelerometers stayed Healthy with all three IMUs, because the fault was
in the gyro only, and that is why the channels are voted separately. After
the fault cleared, IMU1 came back after about 200 ms.

H.5 Barometers: two sensors cannot isolate a fault
--------------------------------------------------
With two barometers disagreeing by more than 2 m, there is no majority.
The code does NOT guess: the altitude is invalid and the status Ambiguous,
for as long as they disagree. Neither is blamed or excluded
(prep_vote_two_baros_disagree_ambiguous_neither_blamed).
One barometer missing -> Degraded, using the other.
When a barometer returns after an absence, it must agree 10 times before it
contributes, so the output cannot jump. The frame raises
quality::BaroSourceChanged once at the moment the contributing set changes.
A consumer can then expect a step of up to the 2 m tolerance.

H.6 What voting can NOT do (say this in the review)
---------------------------------------------------
Majority voting assumes faults are independent. If two IMUs fail the SAME
way (shared supply glitch, shared SPI fault, same temperature error), they
out-vote the healthy third.
prep_e2e_two_imus_fail_same_way_common_mode_limit demonstrates exactly this.
The healthy IMU2 gets EXCLUDED, and the output follows the faulty pair. The
voter detects INCONSISTENCY; it cannot prove CORRECTNESS. The mitigations
are hardware-level (separate supplies/buses, different part numbers) or
physics-level (cross-checks against GNSS and the barometer in the future
estimator).

H.7 Tolerances (all in ProcessingConfig.hpp, none tuned)
--------------------------------------------------------
  accelerometer: 1.0 m/s^2 + 5 %
     (offsets of tens of mg; lever arm w^2 r = 0.5 m/s^2 at 5 rad/s with 2 cm
      between chips; sample-time skew)
  gyro:          0.05 rad/s + 5 % (after calibration)
  barometer:     2.0 m
Re-derive these from measured IMU-to-IMU differences on the real board.

H.8 Memory, timing, tests
-------------------------
  88 B per voter, 3 voters. update() is about 1.7 KB of code. Its work is
  fixed: 3 pairs, 3 x 3 flags.
  16 unit tests + 7 end-to-end tests (see K).

J. FREERTOS TASK INTEGRATION
===============================================================================

Files: Components/Tasks/SensorProcessingTask.hpp / .cpp
       Components/Tasks/TaskConfig.hpp (lines 44-71)

J.1 Why a separate task
-----------------------
SensorAcquisitionTask must keep its 10 ms rhythm (priority 40). If
calibration, alignment, voting and pow() ran inside it, a slow path could
delay a sensor read. SensorProcessingTask runs at priority 32: below
acquisition, so it can never preempt a sensor read, and above the health
monitor (16). Both orderings are enforced at compile time with static_assert
(TaskConfig.hpp lines 69-70).

J.2 Data path
-------------
At start-up, before the scheduler runs (RunInterface.cpp line 56):
  queue_.create()                                   static FreeRTOS queue, 64 x 88 B
  hub.subscribe(&queue_, OverflowPolicy::DropOldest)
  xTaskCreateStatic(... "SensorProc", 1024 words, priority 32 ...)
  health.watchTask(processing.handle(), "SensorProc")   (line 61)
Every acquisition cycle: hub.publish(msg) copies the message into this queue
(0-tick send, never blocks). If the queue is full, the OLDEST queued
message is dropped to make room (DropOldest). The hub counts it, and the
pipeline sees it as a sequence gap.

J.3 The loop (SensorProcessingTask::Run, from line 54)
------------------------------------------------------
  line 57: pipeline_.start(now) -> the start-up calibration session begins
  forever:
    line 63: popWait(msg, 50 ms)  BLOCKS (no CPU used) until a message arrives
                                  or 50 ms pass
    line 66: if another task called requestCalibration(), start a session
             (a std::atomic<bool> flag, lock-free on Cortex-M7)
    ingest the first message, then drain the rest (line 76), at most 64 per
             wake: a bounded loop even if the producer keeps publishing
    line 84: buildFrame()
    line 85: frames_.publish(frame) into the ProcessedFrameMailbox
    update stats: batches, messages, idle timeouts, largest batch,
                  last/max processing time (1 ms resolution)
Because acquisition has the higher priority, the processing task normally
wakes right after an acquisition cycle ends, finds that whole cycle's burst
(about 6-10 messages) in the queue, and produces ONE frame per cycle.
If NO data arrives for 50 ms, it still produces a frame. That frame says
ImuUnavailable / NoNewImuData etc., so a dead sensor bus is VISIBLE instead
of silently freezing the last frame.

J.4 Choosing the numbers
------------------------
  Priority 32   below acquisition (40), above defaultTask (24) and health (16).
  Queue 64      production is about 575 messages/s = about 6 per 10 ms (RTOS_TIMING_ANALYSIS.md
                section 7). 64 slots cover about 110 ms, i.e. about 10 cycles of
                processing delay before DropOldest acts. Cost: 64 x 88 = 5.6 KB of static RAM.
  Stack 1024 words (4 KB). The compiler's -fstack-usage reports the deepest
                chain as Run 152 B -> fillImu 320 B -> RedundancyVoter::update 104 B,
                about 0.65 KB plus the RTOS context frame. The margin is large, so
                confirm with the Health task's stack high-water mark on hardware
                and shrink later if wanted.
  Idle 50 ms    long enough to never trigger while sensors run, short enough
                that a total data loss shows up within 5 cycles.

J.5 Thread safety
-----------------
  * The pipeline is touched ONLY by the processing task. No locks are needed.
  * ProcessedFrameMailbox copies the frame in and out inside
    taskENTER_CRITICAL (RtosCriticalSection). The copy is 420 B, estimated
    at a few microseconds. Interrupts at priority >= 5 wait meanwhile. This
    is acceptable, but should be measured; a double buffer is the
    alternative.
  * HealthMonitorTask reads the task's counters (each one aligned 32-bit
    word) and copies the frame through the mailbox into a MEMBER variable
    (HealthMonitorTask.cpp line 127), not onto its 2 KB stack.
  * TSan test prep_concurrency_frame_mailbox_never_torn: one writer, one
    reader, 20 000 frames. The reader never saw a half-written frame, and
    the sequence never went backwards.

J.6 What the health monitor now shows (g_systemHealth, debugger Live Expressions)
---------------------------------------------------------------------------------
processingBatches, processingMessages, processingIdleTimeouts,
processingMaxBatch, processingLast/MaxTimeMs, frameSequence, frameAgeMs,
frameQualityFlags, calibrationState / Failure / SessionId,
accelStatus / gyroStatus / baroStatus (RedundancyStatus numbers:
0 Unavailable, 1 Healthy, 2 Degraded, 3 Ambiguous), contributing masks,

------------------

K. SIMULATION AND TESTING
===============================================================================

K.1 The simulator (Tests/sim/SensorSimulator.hpp)
-------------------------------------------------
It produces the same SensorMessage stream the acquisition task would:
per-sensor rates and phases (barometer 1 offset by 10 ms, as in reality),
per-sensor sequence numbers, and MCU timestamps.
  * Truth is defined in the BODY frame as functions of time: angularRate(t),
    specificForce(t), altitude(t), referencePressure(t) (weather).
  * Each IMU sees the truth through the TRANSPOSE of its mounting matrix,
    then adds its own bias, offset and noise. So the test checks that the
    pipeline's alignment undoes a rotation it did not create itself.
  * Pressure is generated with the INVERSE formula,
    P = P0 (1 - h/44330)^(1/0.1903). The pipeline's forward formula is
    therefore checked against an independent expression, not against
    itself.
  * Noise comes from its own xorshift64* generator plus Box-Muller.
    std::normal_distribution is NOT used, because its output differs between
    C++ standard libraries, and every run must give identical numbers.
  * Faults: invalid flag, NaN, saturation, step bias (gyroFaultBias /
    accelFaultBias / faultPa), missing reads (no sequence step), LOST
    samples (sequence consumed, message not delivered), delayed and
    jittered timestamps, random outliers, absent sensors, and recovery
    (toggle any of these between runs).
  * Scheduling is relative to the first step. (A bug found and fixed:
    "now % 10" breaks across the 2^32 wrap because 2^32 is not a multiple
    of 10.)

K.2 The test suites (85 new tests, all 205 pass)
------------------------------------------------
  validation 15 | calibration and stationary 27 | alignment 9 | redundancy 16 | pipeline/e2e 18
Each module has unit tests; the pipeline file is the integration and

L. FUTURE INTEGRATION WITH MADGWICK AND UKF
===============================================================================

L.1 Madgwick (attitude) - what it will read
-------------------------------------------
  angularRate_radps.value   body FRD, bias removed      (use only if valid)
  gyroDtMs / gyroDtValid    integrate only if valid; dt = gyroDtMs / 1000
  specificForce_mps2.value  body FRD, gravity included  (its direction gives "down" when not accelerating)
  mag.fieldBody_uT          optional; NOT iron-calibrated yet (MagNotIronCalibrated)
It produces R_nb (body -> NED) or a quaternion.
The frame does NOT remove gravity. That needs the attitude, which is
Madgwick's job. Free fall (|f| about 0) must be handled there: the
accelerometer carries no attitude information then.

L.2 UKF (vertical / navigation) - what it will read
---------------------------------------------------
  baroAltitude_m (relative, positive up, drifts with weather; check
                  status and BaroSourceChanged)
  vertical acceleration: (R_nb x specificForce)_down + g, computed AFTER
                  Madgwick (not here)
  gnss.* (heightMsl, velocities, accuracies), in their own time domain
  lidar.distance_m (along the sensor boresight; tilt-correct with attitude
                  before using it as height above ground)
To convert to ENU, use pg::nedToEnu() (kEnuFromNed) only.

L.3 How to hand frames to a new consumer
----------------------------------------
Simplest: poll SensorProcessingTask::Inst().frames().read(frame) and use
frame.sequence to see whether it is new, and how many frames were missed.
If a filter must see EVERY frame, add a bounded queue implementing
IProcessedFrameSink in the processing task. The producer side does not
change.

L.4 What is deliberately NOT here
---------------------------------
No Madgwick, no Kalman/UKF, no aerodynamic model, no crash detection, no
landing guidance, no SOS logic. These were excluded by the brief.

===============================================================================
APPENDIX 3. LIKELY REVIEW QUESTIONS
===============================================================================
Q: Why not average all three IMUs?
A: An average lets one bad sensor pull the result: a 3 m/s^2 fault moves a
   3-way mean by 1 m/s^2. Voting identifies it and leaves it out (H.4).
Q: Why the mean and not the median when all three agree?
A: When all three agree within tolerance, the mean has the lowest noise
   (noise shrinks by sqrt(3)). The median is used only for the "chain"
   case, where it is more robust.
Q: Two barometers disagree. Why not pick the one closer to the GNSS?
A: That is fusion, the UKF's job. Preprocessing reports Ambiguous honestly
   and lets the estimator decide with more information.
Q: Why must the gyro be calibrated before any angular rate is output?
A: An uncalibrated MEMS gyro can be off by around a degree per second;
   integrated, that is about 60 deg of heading error per minute. The flag
   requireGyroCalibration can be set false for experiments, and the frame
   then still says GyroNotCalibrated.
Q: What if the wearer never stands still for 5 s?
A: There is no gyro output until they do (the frame says so). The session
   completes at the first quiet 5 s and retries every 60 s otherwise.
   Putting the module down at launch is the practical procedure; it must be
   validated with real users.
Q: Is 5 s enough?
A: Bias error = sigma / sqrt(n): 500 samples give about 0.004 dps for
   sigma = 0.086 dps. That is far below what a few minutes of flight needs.
   The limiting factor will be temperature drift during flight, which
   needs a temperature model or in-flight estimation later.
Q: Why FRD and not ENU for the body frame?
A: ENU is a NAVIGATION frame, not a body frame. FRD pairs with NED in the
   standard attitude formulation, and the one-line NED->ENU matrix keeps
   the project's ENU navigation outputs unambiguous.
