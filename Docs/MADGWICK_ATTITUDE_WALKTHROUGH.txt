---------------------------------------------------------------------
A. WHAT ORIENTATION MEANS
---------------------------------------------------------------------

"Orientation" (also "attitude") is the answer to: how is the device turned
relative to the world? It is NOT position (where it is) and NOT velocity.

Concretely we need three things for ParaGuardian:
  1. Which way is UP, seen from the device. This gives roll and pitch, also
     called "tilt". Without it we cannot tell the difference between "the
     pilot is accelerating upward" and "the device is tilted so gravity
     shows up on a different axis".
  2. Which way is NORTH, seen from the device. This gives heading (yaw).
     Only needed for navigation-type uses; vertical acceleration does not
     need it.
  3. A way to convert any vector measured by the device (acceleration,
     magnetic field) into world coordinates.

Item 3 is the real purpose. Mathematically, an orientation IS a rotation:
the rotation that turns the device's axes into the world's axes. We store
it as a unit quaternion (section C) and can also express it as a 3x3
rotation matrix R or, for humans, as roll/pitch/yaw angles.

The attitude filter's main product for the next milestone is the VERTICAL
LINEAR ACCELERATION: how fast the pilot is accelerating up or down,
with gravity removed. The future UKF combines it with the barometer.

B. COORDINATE SYSTEMS
---------------------------------------------------------------------

B.1 The body frame (from Milestone 2, unchanged)
  File: Components/Processing/AxisAlignment.hpp
  FRD = Forward, Right, Down:
     +x forward (where the pilot faces)
     +y right
     +z down
  It is right-handed: x cross y = z (forward x right = down; use your right
  hand: index finger forward, middle finger right, thumb points down).
  Every vector in ProcessedSensorFrame (specificForce_mps2,
  angularRate_radps, mag.fieldBody_uT) is in FRD.

B.2 The navigation frame: ENU
  East, North, Up:
     +x east
     +y north
     +z up
  Also right-handed (east x north = up).

B.3 Why two frames that "disagree" are fine
  FRD has z DOWN, ENU has z UP. That looks contradictory but it is not:
  they are different frames, and the attitude quaternion is exactly the
  rotation between them. When the device is level and faces north:
     body +x (forward) = ENU +y (north)
     body +y (right)   = ENU +x (east)
     body +z (down)    = ENU -z
  You can check this in the test att_quat_euler_matches_independent_matrix_
  and_physical_meaning (Tests/unit/test_attitude_math.cpp), which asserts
  exactly these three mappings.

B.3a NED, and why it appears at all
  Aerospace roll/pitch/yaw angles are traditionally defined for an FRD body
  relative to NED (North, East, Down). We only use NED to PRINT angles that
  pilots recognise. The relation NED <-> ENU is fixed:
     east = NED y, north = NED x, up = -NED z
  As a rotation this is 180 degrees about the axis halfway between north
  and east. Its quaternion is
     quat::kEnuFromNed = (w 0, x 0.7071, y 0.7071, z 0)
  (Quaternion.hpp line 61). It is the same matrix as kEnuFromNed in
  AxisAlignment.hpp (test att_quat_enu_from_ned_matches_axis_alignment_
  matrix). It is its own inverse: doing it twice gets you back.

B.4 Specific force vs gravity (the most common sign mistake)
  An accelerometer does NOT measure gravity. It measures SPECIFIC FORCE:
       f = a - g_vec
  where a is the true acceleration and g_vec = (0, 0, -9.80665) in ENU.
  Sitting still on a table: a = 0, so f = -g_vec = (0, 0, +9.80665): the
  accelerometer reports +g UPWARD, because the table pushes up on it.
  In FRD body axes, level, that same vector is (0, 0, -9.80665), because
  body z points down.
  In free fall: a = g_vec, so f = 0. The accelerometer reads zero.
  This is why "accelerometer z" is not "vertical acceleration".

C. QUATERNIONS FROM FIRST PRINCIPLES
---------------------------------------------------------------------

C.1 Why not just angles?
  Roll/pitch/yaw are three numbers, but they have two problems:
  - order matters (rotate about x then y is not y then x), so every formula
    depends on a convention that is easy to get wrong;
  - at pitch = +-90 degrees ("gimbal lock") roll and yaw become the same
    motion and the equations divide by zero.
  A 3x3 rotation matrix has no singularity, but it has 9 numbers that must
  stay orthonormal (6 constraints), which drifts with rounding.
  A unit quaternion has 4 numbers and 1 constraint (length 1). No
  singularities, cheap to keep normalised, cheap to multiply.

C.2 What a quaternion is
  q = w + x i + y j + z k, with i^2 = j^2 = k^2 = ijk = -1 (Hamilton).
  Stored scalar-first in struct Quaternion { float w, x, y, z; }
  (Quaternion.hpp line 34).
  A rotation by angle theta about unit axis u is
       q = ( cos(theta/2),  u * sin(theta/2) )
  See quat::fromAxisAngle (line 131). Example: 90 degrees about ENU up
  (0,0,1): q = (0.7071, 0, 0, 0.7071). It turns east (1,0,0) into north
  (0,1,0) (test att_quat_axis_angle_right_hand_rule).

  Note the HALF angle. Two consequences:
  - q and -q are the SAME rotation (theta and theta + 360 deg). The filter
    keeps w >= 0 only for readability (MadgwickFilter.cpp, end of update).
  - a correction that changes q by a small amount d changes the rotation
    angle by about 2*d. This factor 2 appears in section F.

C.3 What OUR quaternion means (memorise this sentence)
  q = q_ENU_from_body: it takes a vector written in BODY coordinates and
  gives the same vector written in ENU coordinates:
       v_ENU = q (x) [0, v_body] (x) q*      ("(x)" = Hamilton product,
                                              q* = conjugate (w, -x, -y, -z))
  quat::rotate(q, v) (line 106) computes that efficiently:
       t = 2 (q_v x v);   v' = v + w t + q_v x t
  quat::toMatrix(q) (line 120) gives the equivalent matrix R(q) with
  v_ENU = R v_body. Its COLUMNS are the body axes expressed in ENU; its ROWS
  are the ENU axes expressed in body coordinates. Row 3 = "up" in body
  axes. Keep that in mind for section F.

  The inverse direction (ENU -> body) uses the conjugate:
  quat::rotateInverse (line 117).

C.4 Chaining
  Frames chain like matrices: q_A_from_C = q_A_from_B (x) q_B_from_C.
  Example used in the code: q_ENU_from_body = q_ENU_from_NED (x)
  q_NED_from_body (quat::fromEulerNedZyx, line 146).
  The test att_quat_hamilton_product_composes_like_matrices checks, for 200
  random pairs, that the matrix of a(x)b equals R(a)*R(b), using a matrix
  formula written separately in Tests/sim/AttitudeSimulator.hpp.

C.5 Euler angles for display (quat::toEulerNedZyx, line 161)
  roll  = atan2(R_NED[2][1], R_NED[2][2])
  pitch = -asin(R_NED[2][0])      (argument clamped to [-1, 1])
  yaw   = atan2(R_NED[1][0], R_NED[0][0])
  with R_NED = matrix of (q_ENU_from_NED (x) q). Sequence Z-Y-X (yaw, then
  pitch, then roll). Signs: roll + = right side down; pitch + = nose up;
  yaw = heading clockwise from north (0 north, 90 east).
  These are ONLY for display and diagnostics (AttitudeEstimate::eulerDeg,
  g_systemHealth.rollCentiDeg, ...). The filter never uses them.

D. WHY ACCELEROMETERS CANNOT DETERMINE YAW
---------------------------------------------------------------------

At rest the accelerometer tells you the direction of "up" in body axes:
two angles (roll, pitch). Now spin the device about the vertical axis.
"Up" does not change, so the accelerometer reading does not change. Every
heading gives the same measurement: yaw is NOT OBSERVABLE from gravity.

Consequences in the code:
  - AttitudeEstimator::level() (AttitudeEstimator.cpp line 156) computes
    roll and pitch from the averaged specific force and sets yaw = 0 (the
    first time) or keeps the previous yaw (after a re-initialisation).
    "Yaw 0" just means body x points to a "pseudo-north" that is wherever
    the device was facing. The output says so: heading = Unreferenced,
    flag HeadingUnreferenced, yawReferenced = false.
  - In 6-axis mode (gyro + accel) yaw is only integrated from the gyro and
    drifts with the gyro bias. Simulation S8: a 0.01 rad/s bias drifts yaw
    by 68.5 degrees in 2 minutes while tilt stays below 0.35 degrees.
  - Only a magnetometer (or GNSS track) can provide absolute heading.

Levelling formulas (FRD body, at rest f = g*(sin th, -sin ph cos th,
-cos ph cos th)):
     roll  = atan2(-fy, -fz)
     pitch = atan2(fx, sqrt(fy^2 + fz^2))
Example: device rolled 30 degrees right, pitch 0:
  f_body = (0, -9.80665*sin30, -9.80665*cos30) = (0, -4.903, -8.493)
  roll = atan2(4.903, 8.493) = 30.0 degrees. Test
  att_est_levels_roll_pitch_from_gravity_yaw_unreferenced levels at roll 30
  / pitch -20 with realistic noise: tilt error 0.109 degrees.

E. GYROSCOPE INTEGRATION AND DRIFT
---------------------------------------------------------------------

The gyroscope measures angular rate omega (rad/s) in body axes. The
orientation changes as
       dq/dt = 1/2 * q (x) [0, omega_body]
The body rate multiplies on the RIGHT because it is measured in the body
frame (test att_madgwick_pure_gyro_integration_matches_truth starts at yaw
0.7 rad and rolls about the BODY x axis; the result matches the exact
truth within 0.02 degrees).

Discrete step (MadgwickFilter::update, MadgwickFilter.cpp line 68):
       q_new = normalise(q + 1/2 q (x) [0, omega] * dt)

Numerical example (computed with the real code):
  start level, yaw 0:  q = (0, 0.707107, 0.707107, 0)
  gyro (0.5, 0, 0) rad/s for dt = 0.010 s (rolling right)
  -> q = (0.001768, -0.707105, -0.707105, 0.001768)
     (sign flipped to keep w >= 0: same rotation)
  roll = 0.2865 degrees = 0.5 rad/s * 0.01 s. Correct.

Where dt comes from: the timestamps (section K/L), not an assumed 10 ms.

Why it drifts: any error in omega (residual bias b, noise, scale factor) is
integrated. A constant bias b gives an angle error growing as b*t. After
the Milestone 2 calibration the simulated residual is about 2e-4 rad/s
(0.7 degrees per minute). A real gyro whose bias moves with temperature
can be much worse. The gyro is excellent short-term and poor long-term;
the accelerometer (gravity) is poor short-term (noise, accelerations) and
good long-term. Combining them is the job of the filter.

F. HOW MADGWICK CORRECTIONS WORK
---------------------------------------------------------------------

Reference: S. Madgwick, "An efficient orientation filter for inertial and
inertial/magnetic sensor arrays", 2010. File: MadgwickFilter.cpp.

F.1 The idea in one sentence
  Ask "which small change of q would make the predicted direction of
  gravity match the measured one?", and nudge q that way at a fixed rate
  beta, while the gyro does the fast tracking.

F.2 The error function (accelGradient, line 23)
  Predicted up in body axes = R(q)^T * (0,0,1) = third row of R:
     ( 2(q1q3 - q0q2), 2(q0q1 + q2q3), 1 - 2q1^2 - 2q2^2 )
  Measured up = a_hat = f / |f| (unit specific force).
  Error vector f_g = predicted - measured (3 numbers).
  We want to minimise E = 1/2 |f_g|^2 over q. Its gradient with respect to
  the four quaternion numbers is J^T f_g, where J is the 3x4 matrix of
  partial derivatives (the Jacobian) written out in the code:
     J_g = [[-2q2,  2q3, -2q0, 2q1],
            [ 2q1,  2q0,  2q3, 2q2],
            [   0, -4q1, -4q2,   0]]

F.3 The update
     qdot = 1/2 q (x) [0, omega]  -  beta * grad / |grad|
     q    = normalise(q + qdot * dt)
  The gradient is NORMALISED, so the correction always has size beta,
  whatever the size of the error. In rotation angle that is 2*beta rad/s
  (the factor 2 from C.2).

F.4 Worked example (real numbers from the code)
  Truth: level. Estimate: rolled 2 degrees.
    q          = (-0.012341, 0.706999, 0.706999, -0.012341)
    predicted up in body = (0, -0.034899, -0.999391)
    measured  up a_hat   = (0, 0, -1)          (f = (0, 0, -9.80665))
    angle between them   = 2.0000 degrees      (accelErrorDeg)
    f_g  = (0, -0.034899, 0.000609)
    grad = (-0.049348, -0.000862, -0.000862, -0.049348), |grad| = 0.0698
  One step, beta = 0.1 rad/s, dt = 0.01 s, gyro 0:
    roll after = 1.8854 degrees: it moved 0.1146 degrees
             = 2 * beta * dt = 2 * 0.1 * 0.01 rad = 0.1146 degrees. Exactly.

F.5 How we know the gradient formulas are right
  Test att_madgwick_accel_gradient_matches_finite_difference_and_vanishes_
  at_truth: for 100 random orientations it computes the gradient by
  numerical differentiation of an independently written error function
  (in double precision) and compares; it also checks the gradient is zero
  when the estimate equals the truth. Same for the magnetometer gradient.

F.6 What beta does (MADGWICK_VALIDATION_REPORT.md section 6)
  - Larger beta: cancels larger gyro errors (up to 2*beta rad/s) and
    re-converges faster, but follows accelerometer noise and false
    "gravity" during accelerations more.
  - The steady-state wobble ("limit cycle") is 2*beta*dt: 0.115 degrees
    at beta 0.1 (measured 0.115).
  - Default beta = 0.1 rad/s (Madgwick's reference default). The earlier
    proposal 0.5 is NOT used: 5x the wobble, 2.4x the noise, no benefit in
    any simulated case. This is NOT the same number as the "gain 0.5" of
    the Fusion library, which is a different update law (design doc 2.1).
  - Nothing proves 0.1 is optimal for paragliding; it must be tuned on
    recorded flight data.

G. ACCELEROMETER REJECTION
---------------------------------------------------------------------

The correction in F assumes the accelerometer measures only gravity. In
flight it also measures turns, turbulence and pitch changes. A correction
using those would tilt the estimate. So AttitudeEstimator::decideAccel
(AttitudeEstimator.cpp line 223) checks, in this order:

  1. Valid and fresh?           else Unavailable
  2. | |f| - g | <= 1.0 m/s^2?   else RejectedMagnitude (free fall: |f| ~ 0)
  3. |omega| <= 0.10 rad/s?     else RejectedRotation ("turn gate")
  4. angle(a_hat, predicted up) <= 10 degrees?  else RejectedDirection
     (unless the recovery rule applies, section K)

Rejected means: this step is gyro-only (beta 0). The estimate keeps
propagating; nothing is reset.

G.1 Why the magnitude test alone is not enough
  In a coordinated turn at bank phi the pilot feels g/cos(phi) along the
  seat: |f| = 10.15 m/s^2 at 15 degrees, only 0.34 above g, which passes.
  But its direction is tilted by phi from true vertical.

G.2 Why the angle test alone is not enough (a finding of this milestone)
  In a coordinated turn, specific force is ALWAYS along body -z (that is
  what "coordinated" means: no sideways force). If the estimate is right,
  the angle test sees the bank angle and rejects. But during a gradual
  roll-in, while the bank is still under 10 degrees, every accepted step
  pulls the estimate toward "wings level"; the estimate then never sees a
  big error, and the gate never triggers. Simulation (validation report
  7.1): with any angle gate, a sustained 15-degree turn ends with the FULL
  15-degree error.

G.3 The turn gate
  A turn at bank phi and airspeed V rotates at g*tan(phi)/V
  (0.26 rad/s at 15 degrees and 10 m/s). Rejecting corrections while
  |omega| > 0.10 rad/s removes most of the problem:
     30-degree turn: 20.05 -> 1.16 degrees max tilt error
     15-degree turn: 15.27 -> 1.63 degrees
  What it costs (be ready to say this in a review):
     - slower turns (< 0.10 rad/s, about 6 degrees of bank at 10 m/s)
       still leak their whole bank angle (5-degree turn: 5.24 degrees);
     - in turbulence corrections are accepted only ~4 % of the time, so
       the attitude is mostly gyro-only. With a good gyro that is fine
       (simulation), with a drifting gyro it is not (unknown until
       hardware);
     - the better long-term answer is to subtract the turn acceleration
       omega x v using GNSS velocity (future milestone).

G.4 Degraded state
  If no correction has been accepted for 2 s (degradedAfterMs), the state
  becomes Degraded and the flag AccelRejectedProlonged is set. The
  orientation is still output, but linear acceleration is marked "valid,
  not reliable". During the simulated 30-degree turn the estimator reports
  Degraded, as it should.


---------------------------------------------------------------------
H. MAGNETOMETER HANDLING
---------------------------------------------------------------------

File: AttitudeEstimator.cpp, decideMag (line 280); MadgwickFilter
magGradient / magReference.

H.1 Why the default is "do not use it"
  A magnetometer measures Earth's field PLUS fields from nearby metal and
  currents (hard iron) and distortions (soft iron). Without a calibration
  done in the final enclosure, heading from it is not trustworthy.
  ParaGuardian has no such calibration yet: frame.mag.ironCalibrated is
  always false. The default policy MagPolicy::CalibratedOnly therefore
  never uses it -> 6-axis, heading Unreferenced. Policies:
     Disabled           never
     CalibratedOnly     only if the frame says calibrated (default)
     AllowUncalibrated  experimental; heading reported "MagneticUnvalidated"

H.2 Four separate facts, four separate outputs
  measurement valid (input magValid) / calibration valid (magCalibrated) /
  correction accepted (magCorrection) / heading reliable
  (heading == MagneticReferenced, yawReferenced). Never collapsed into one
  boolean.

H.3 The magnetometer Madgwick term (ENU version)
  Madgwick's paper uses a world frame with x = north. In ENU, north is +y.
  The reference field is rebuilt from the measurement at every step:
     h = R(q) m_hat (measured field in ENU)
     b = (0, sqrt(h_e^2 + h_n^2), h_u)
  i.e. same dip angle as measured, horizontal part pointing exactly north.
  The error R(q)^T b - m_hat then only "sees" heading error (mostly). The
  Jacobian was derived for this ENU form (design doc 4.3) and verified by
  finite differences.

H.4 Gates
  1. |m| outside 20..70 uT -> RejectedMagnitude
  2. Acquisition (first use after levelling): 10 DISTINCT consistent
     samples, then ONE yaw-only rotation so the field points north. (A bug
     caught before testing: the 50 Hz magnetometer is seen by two 100 Hz
     gyro steps, so the counter must only count new timestamps.)
  3. Strength changed > 15 % or dip changed > 10 degrees -> rejected
     (typical magnetic disturbance signatures)
  4. Heading disagreement > 10 degrees -> rejected; if the disagreement is
     self-consistent and lasts 5 s -> accepted with the recovery gain.
  5. Only applied in steps where the accelerometer was ALSO accepted (the
     magnetometer gradient can tilt roll/pitch when the field dips).

H.5 Results (validation report section 8)
  acquisition error 0.417 degrees; 3 s 60-degree disturbance: heading error
  never above 0.333 degrees; single wild sample: < 0.2 degrees movement.
  Honest trade-off: a PERSISTENT disturbance that keeps the same strength
  and dip (e.g. a magnet fixed next to the sensor) is accepted after 5 s
  and pulls the heading (42 degrees within 7 s in the test).
  Heading is MAGNETIC, not true north (no declination applied).


---------------------------------------------------------------------
I. QUATERNION NORMALISATION AND NUMERICAL STABILITY
---------------------------------------------------------------------

Only a unit quaternion is a rotation. Adding qdot*dt makes |q| slightly
different from 1, so every step renormalises.
quat::normalize (Quaternion.hpp line 88):
  - refuses non-finite input (NaN, Inf);
  - refuses |q| <= 1e-6 (no direction information left);
  - on refusal leaves the output untouched and returns false.
MadgwickFilter::update returns false in that case and KEEPS the previous
orientation (it never writes garbage into the state); the estimator counts
a numerical fault and declares the orientation Invalid
(AttitudeEstimator::stepTracking). Inputs are checked before use (finite
gyro, positive finite dt and beta), so this path is not expected to occur.

Precision: everything is float (single precision, hardware FPU), except the
levelling average which accumulates in double. First-order integration
error per 10 ms step is about theta^3/12 (3e-7 rad at 180 deg/s).

The gravity compensator has its own policy for quaternions given to it
(GravityCompensator.cpp): within 1 +- 0.01 -> renormalise (round-off);
further -> reject (corruption must not be "fixed" silently).

Tests: att_quat_normalize_rejects_degenerate_and_scales_valid,
att_madgwick_rejects_bad_step_and_keeps_state,
att_gravity_unnormalized_quaternion_policy, and the "never NaN, never
falsely healthy" monitor in every simulation scenario.


---------------------------------------------------------------------
J. GRAVITY COMPENSATION
---------------------------------------------------------------------

File: GravityCompensator.hpp/.cpp. One line of physics:
       a_ENU = R(q) * f_body + (0, 0, -g)
because f = a - g_vec  =>  a = f + g_vec, and g_vec = (0, 0, -g) in ENU.

Worked example (from the real code): device rolled 30 degrees, pilot
accelerating UP at 1 m/s^2.
  f_body          = (0.0000, -5.4033, -9.3588)
  R(q) * f_body   = (0, 0, 10.8066)
  a_ENU           = (0, 0, 1.0000)        vertical +1.0000 m/s^2. Correct.
  The naive "accelerometer z" would be -9.3588: meaningless as vertical
  acceleration. This is why we need the attitude.

Validity rules:
  - orientation invalid, accel invalid, NaN anywhere -> valid = false and
    the numbers are NaN, never 0 (0 would look like "no acceleration").
  - attitude Degraded -> valid but NOT reliable (flag LinearAccelUnreliable).
  - heading does not matter for the vertical component, so 6-axis is fine.

Sensitivity (tests): 1 degree of tilt error -> 0.171 m/s^2 horizontal error,
0.0015 m/s^2 vertical. Accelerometer bias goes straight through (not
calibrated yet).

Simulated free fall: vertical reads -9.81 within 0.054 m/s^2 for 1.5 s.
Stationary: |a_lin| below 0.088 m/s^2. 30-degree turn: vertical error RMS
0.060 m/s^2 (without the rejection logic: max 1.4 m/s^2).

Nothing is integrated into velocity or altitude in this milestone.


---------------------------------------------------------------------
K. FILTER INITIALISATION AND RECOVERY
---------------------------------------------------------------------

K.1 States (AttitudeTypes.hpp line 26)
  Uninitialized  no calibrated gyro or no accelerometer yet
  Initializing   collecting a quasi-static window
  Tracking6Axis  gyro + accepted gravity corrections
  Tracking9Axis  + accepted magnetometer corrections
  Degraded       still propagating, but corrections absent for 2 s, a gap
                 not yet confirmed, or recovery running
  Invalid        orientation discarded (long gap / numerical failure)

K.2 Levelling (stepInit, line 104; level, line 156)
  Needs a CONTINUOUS window of >= 500 ms and >= 40 gyro samples with
  |omega| <= 0.10 rad/s and | |f| - g | <= 0.6 m/s^2. Any motion or a hole
  > 50 ms restarts it. Then roll/pitch from the window's mean specific
  force (section D), yaw 0 or the previous yaw.
  The gyro must be bias-calibrated (Milestone 2, 5 s still at power-on).
  If the wearer moves from power-on, the estimator stays Uninitialized and
  says GyroNotCalibrated: no attitude is invented
  (test att_e2e_moving_at_startup_never_fabricates_attitude).
  "Quasi-static" includes steady straight flight, so re-levelling works in
  the air after an Invalid.

K.3 Gaps (stepTracking, line 387)
  dt <= 50 ms        integrate normally
  50 < dt <= 500 ms  NOT integrated (the rotation during the hole is
                     unknown); mark GapDetected / Degraded; confirmed by the
                     next accepted gravity correction
  > 500 ms           Invalid; re-level when quasi-static data return

K.4 Recovery (decideAccel, the part after the angle test)
  If the estimate is far off (after a gap, or rejected for 5 s), the angle
  gate would reject forever. Recovery FORCES corrections, with the bigger
  gain recoveryBeta = 0.5, but ONLY if the motion has been quasi-static
  for 1 s (|omega| <= 0.10, | |f| - g | <= 0.3). Why not just a timer
  (Fusion style)? In a long spiral dive a timer would align "up" with the
  centrifugal "up": an error as big as the bank angle. Requiring calm
  evidence avoids that.
  Test S14: gyro lost for 300 ms in the middle of a 90-degree roll ->
  50.4 degrees error; recovery brings it under 2 degrees and back to
  Tracking 2.97 s later.

Files: Components/Tasks/SensorProcessingTask.hpp/.cpp,

       SystemHealth.hpp.

L.1 Where it runs
  SensorProcessingTask::Run() already did: wait for sensor messages ->
  ingest them -> buildFrame -> publish the frame. Two lines were added:
       attitude_.update(pg::attitudeInputFromFrame(work_));
       attitudeOut_.publish(attitude_.latest());
  Same task, priority 32, below the acquisition task (40), so the attitude
  maths can never delay a sensor read. No new task, queue or stack.
  Reasons (design doc 11.1): one frame in, one estimate out; ~25-80 us
  estimated per update; a separate task would only add memory, copies and
  latency.

L.2 One step per NEW gyro sample
  The task builds a frame every time it wakes, including the 50 ms idle
  timeout when no data arrived. The estimator compares the frame's gyro
  timestamp with the last integrated one: equal -> NotNew (nothing done),
  older -> OutOfOrder. So repeated frames are never integrated twice
  (tests S17 and att_e2e_republished_frames_not_integrated_twice: the final
  quaternion is bit-for-bit identical with and without repeats).
  Barometer, GNSS and LiDAR messages never trigger an attitude step.

L.3 Reading the result from another task
  pg::AttitudeEstimate e;
  if (SensorProcessingTask::Inst().attitude().read(e) && e.orientationValid) {
      // e.q, e.linearAccel (check .valid / .reliable), e.qualityFlags ...
  }
  read() copies under a critical section (164 bytes); the producer never
  blocks. TSan test: 20 000 writes vs concurrent reads, no torn copy.

L.4 Health
  HealthMonitorTask copies the estimate into g_systemHealth once per
  second: attitudeState, headingReference, accel/mag correction status,
  roll/pitch/yaw in centi-degrees, vertical acceleration in mm/s^2,
  counters. INT32_MIN means "not valid". Watch it in CubeIDE Live
  Expressions.

L.5 Memory and time (measured / estimated)
  RAM +816 bytes total, heap 0, stack ~0.5 KB on the existing 4 KB stack,
  flash +11.7 KB (Release). Host: 76 ns per update. STM32: estimated
  25-80 us per update (NOT measured).

M. SIMULATED TEST CASES WITH ACTUAL VALUES
---------------------------------------------------------------------

The simulator (Tests/sim/AttitudeSimulator.hpp) is independent of the
production maths: truth is a double-precision rotation matrix, rotated by
the exact Rodrigues formula every 0.5 ms; accelerometer data are generated
as f = R^T (a + (0,0,g)), so accelerations are physical, never "tilted
gravity". Error = 2*acos(|q_true . q_est|).

0.002 rad/s, accel noise 0.02 m/s^2, mag noise 0.2 uT.

---------------------------------------------------------------------
N. HOW TO EXPLAIN AND DEFEND THE DESIGN IN A CAPSTONE REVIEW
---------------------------------------------------------------------

Likely questions and short, honest answers:

Q: Why Madgwick and not a Kalman filter for attitude?
A: Madgwick is a small, well-published filter with one main tuning
   parameter, constant cost, no matrices to invert, and good accuracy for
   MEMS IMUs. The UKF in the next milestone handles altitude/vertical
   speed, where explicit uncertainty matters more. Attitude errors are
   reported through state, flags and measured simulation errors, not a
   covariance (a known limitation of Madgwick).

Q: Why not reuse Fusion from the SOAR project?
A: The copy has no licence text, includes desktop headers, uses a
   different (revised) update law, counts recovery in samples although our
   dt varies, and the SOAR review found gravity-handling bugs around it.
   The original algorithm is ~150 lines and was verified line by line.

Q: How do you know the signs are right?
A: Independent checks, not "tune until green": level rest maps to +g up in
   ENU; yaw +90 points body x east; nose-up pitch gives an up component;
   gradients match finite differences; the simulator is a separate double-
   precision matrix implementation; and a simulator sign bug (identity =
   upside down) was caught BY the filter levelling to roll 180 degrees.

Q: What happens in a thermal (long turn)?
A: The turn gate stops accelerometer corrections, so the tilt is held by
   the gyro alone; the state shows Degraded after 2 s. With the simulated
   gyro the error stays near 1 degree; with a real drifting gyro it would
   grow at the bias rate. Slow, shallow turns below 0.10 rad/s still leak.
   The planned fix is centripetal compensation with GNSS velocity.

Q: Why is beta 0.1?
A: Sweep: noise grows with beta, drift cancellation needs 2*beta > gyro
   error, acceleration sensitivity grows with beta without gates. 0.1 keeps
   margin for real gyro errors that the simulation does not model. Not
   tuned for paragliding; flight data needed.

Q: Can the output lie?
A: It is designed to say when it cannot be trusted: NaN + invalid flags
   when there is no orientation, Degraded when corrections are missing,
   "valid but not reliable" linear acceleration, unreferenced heading
   without a calibrated magnetometer, and inherited flags such as
   MountingUnverified. It can still be wrong in ways it cannot detect
   (sustained slow turns, persistent magnetic disturbances, bias drift).

Q: Is it real-time on the STM32?
A: Estimated 25-80 us per 10 ms frame (< 1 %), static memory only, no
   blocking, in the existing processing task below acquisition priority.
   Not measured on hardware yet; a GPIO-toggle measurement is planned.

Q: What is NOT done?
A: Hardware tests, mounting verification, magnetometer calibration, gyro
   bias over temperature, in-flight restart handling, centripetal
   compensation, UKF, crash detection, landing guidance, SOS.

APPENDIX 2: THE JOURNEY OF ONE IMU SAMPLE
---------------------------------------------------------------------

t = 7.000 s. The pilot is level and starts climbing at +0.5 m/s^2.

1. SensorAcquisitionTask (priority 40) reads the three LSM6DSRX over SPI.
   Each driver converts to SI units and stamps the read-completion time,
   e.g. IMU0 at 7000 ms: gyro (0.0120, -0.0201, 0.0061) rad/s (mostly its
   bias), accel (0.003, -0.011, -10.307) m/s^2 (= -(g + 0.5) on body z).
   It publishes SensorMessages into the SensorDataHub, which copies them
   into SensorProcessingTask's queue (DropOldest). The acquisition task
   never waits.
2. SensorProcessingTask (priority 32) wakes once the acquisition cycle
   blocks, and ingests every queued message (Milestone 2): validation,
   bias subtraction (calibrated earlier), alignment to FRD, 3-IMU voting.
   buildFrame() produces angularRate_radps ~ (0.0002, -0.0001, 0.0001)
   rad/s with timestampMs 7000 and specificForce ~ (0.00, -0.01, -10.31).
3. attitudeInputFromFrame() copies these fields into an AttitudeInput.
4. AttitudeEstimator::update():
   - gyro valid, calibrated, 1 ms old: OK. dt = 7000 - 6990 = 10 ms.
   - |omega| tiny -> not turning. | |f| - g | = 0.5 m/s^2 <= 1.0 -> the
     magnitude gate passes (a +1.5 m/s^2 climb would be rejected: the
     correction is skipped and that step is gyro-only). Angle to predicted
     up ~0.1 deg -> Accepted. Note: a VERTICAL acceleration does not change
     the direction of f, so accepting it does not tilt the estimate.
   - MadgwickFilter::update integrates omega over 10 ms and moves q by
     at most 2*0.1*0.01 rad towards the measured up.
   - finalize(): state Tracking6Axis, Euler ~ (0, 0, yaw), flags include
     MountingUnverified, AccelNotCalibrated, HeadingUnreferenced.
   - GravityCompensator: R(q) f_body = (0, 0, 10.31); minus g ->
     vertical linear acceleration ~ +0.50 m/s^2, valid and reliable.
5. attitudeOut_.publish() copies the 164-byte estimate into the mailbox.
6. Within 1 s HealthMonitorTask copies it to g_systemHealth:
   verticalAccel_mmps2 ~ 500, attitudeState 2 (Tracking6Axis).
7. Next milestone: the UKF will read linearAccel.vertical_mps2 together with
   the barometric altitude to estimate vertical speed and altitude.

End of document.
